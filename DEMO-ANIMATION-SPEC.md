# Connect Lite — the film

**Spec for the promotional film. 26 September 2026; rewritten 27 September as a journey, with captions and music, no voice.**

Audience: **trainers and centre owners**, not trainees. Length: **about five minutes**. **No narration.** Light music under the whole thing, one track, royalty-free; and **captions** — one short line at a time, on the picture, in the house type. A caption is a selling line: it says what the viewer is looking at and why it matters, in the fewest words that are still true. Half the people who receive this will watch it on mute in a staff room; the captions are the film for them.

Nobody is introduced and nobody's name is zoomed on. The foot of every screen says *designed and built by Ramy* and that is enough.

Every scene names the screen, what the cursor does, the captions in order, and where the picture slows. ◆ marks a **hold** — a selling point where the picture stays a beat longer than the eye needs and the cursor stops. Zoom is written where it matters; everything else is a plain screen at 1280 px.

**The shape of the film is a journey.** One link is sent. The trainer sets the course up. A trainee does a teaching practice. The trainer gives feedback. The trainee reads it and does the written work. The assessor comes. The course ends with its reports — and, right at the end, paper, if anyone wants paper. Start to finish, in the order it happens.

## Everything is the demo — nothing real

Lite lives at **`https://lite.celtaconnect.com/`** — every link in the film shows that address, never `github.io`. Both courses are demo courses in the owner console:

- **`c3` — running.** Elmswood English Centre, twelve candidates, three weeks in: TP1–3 written up and returned, the fourth in progress. Tutors Jordan Blake and Diane Okonkwo; Emily Carter for the trainee side. **Parts 1–5** come from here — everything that shows a course *in motion*.
- **`c4` — finished.** *CELTA — C/16 2026*, the same centre, twelve candidates, eight TPs each, four assignments each, a full end-of-course report for every one. **Parts 6–7** come from here — the assessor, the grades, the reports — because a course three weeks in has none of those yet, and a report that names TP7 on a course at TP3 is what an assessor notices. **Nothing in the film may show a finished document on the running course.**

**The Classroom and Drive frames in scene 2 are demo ones too** — a demo class in Google Classroom and a demo Drive folder made for the film, with the demo course's name on them. Never a real course, never real candidates, never a real folder of Ramy's (Ramy, 27 Sep: *"I thought we should do it with the demo one."*).

Capture from the live links, not from mock-ups. Never paste a link into this file — the repo is public and a link is a key.

## The three things the film must sell

1. **One link, and a course happens inside it.** No accounts, no passwords, no installing. The link lives wherever the course already lives — Classroom, Drive, a browser — and keeps working when the internet drops.
2. **Nothing is typed twice.** The plan becomes the feedback sheet; the feedback becomes the grades; the grades become the assessor's view and the final report. Show the same words travelling.
3. **It looks like this.** The screens are the selling point. Hold on them.

**And one thing it must not say:** that anything is downloaded, exported or sent to anyone. The assessor gets a link. Paper is an *option*, and it comes at the very end (Ramy, 27 Sep: *"The assessor pack does not have anything to do with downloading a PDF. This PDF thing should be at the very end as an option."*).

## Captions — how they are set

One line at a time, never two stacked. House type, on a dark pill at the foot of the picture, the way the first cut did it. Each stays for as long as it takes to read twice, then goes; the next arrives with its beat, not before. A caption never says what the picture already shows — it says why it matters.

## Build notes before filming

- **Make the running demo permanent first.** `c3` as it stands runs 15 Sep → 7 Oct 2026: it reads as finished in October and its assessor link — the same door the offer card carries — dies fourteen days after that. A demo course must have no beginning and no end (Ramy, 27 Sep: *"it doesn't have a beginning and an end, so we could just keep using it"*). The store seals a course's dates only when its first candidate is added, so re-mint the running demo with its end years out *before* the candidates go in, then re-plant it (demo-mint, tag-demo, critLearn seed). Film on that, and point the card's doors at that.
- **Everything else the film shows is built and live** as of 27 Sep: the hero card, the taught criterion chips and their tip, the finished demo course, the domain.
- **Demo Classroom and demo Drive for scene 2** — make them for the film, named for the demo course, nothing real in frame.
- **Dictation — Chrome or Edge on a computer, always.** Safari and every browser on iPhone and iPad hide the *Dictate* button. Film scenes 7, 10 and 12 in Chrome, microphone permitted beforehand so no permission sheet appears.
- **Dictation and the exchange are two features.** Scene 12 is cursor-in-the-box; scene 13 is Copy → outside → Paste. Two scenes, two captions.
- **Printing appears once, in scene 20.** Not in 14, not in 17. The assessor's scene is a scroll of a link, not a download.
- **Offline for scene 2.** The offline shell (`sw.js`) must have cached the pages once before the Wi-Fi goes off; open each page once online before filming.
- **Drive link for scene 8.** A real shared file; the preview needs "anyone with the link".
- **Dictation window for scene 13.** Any model; the brief is what *Copy* puts on the clipboard. If it cannot be shown, cut from *Copy* to *Paste something back*.
- **The PDF for scene 20.** macOS Safari's dialog is cleanest; export to PDF and show the PDF in Preview for the flip and the zoom.
