# Connect Lite — the animation

**Spec for the animated walkthrough. 26 September 2026, revised 27 September.**

Audience: **trainers and centre owners**, not trainees. Length: **4½–5 minutes**. One voice throughout — a trainer's, present tense, first person plural where it fits ("we send one link"). Every scene names the screen, what the cursor does, what the voice says, and where the picture slows.

◆ marks a **hold** — a selling point where the picture stays a beat longer than the eye needs, and the cursor stops moving. Zoom is written where it matters; everything else is a plain screen at 1280 px.

**Two demo courses, and which scenes come from which.** Lite lives at **`https://lite.celtaconnect.com/`** now — every link in the film shows that address, never `github.io`. Both courses are in the owner console:

- **`c3` — running.** Elmswood English Centre, twelve candidates, three weeks in: TP1–3 written up and returned, the fourth in progress. Tutors Jordan Blake and Diane Okonkwo; Emily Carter for the trainee side. **Scenes 1–16** come from here — everything that shows a course *in motion*.
- **`c4` — finished.** *CELTA — C/16 2026*, the same centre, twelve candidates, eight TPs each, four assignments each, and a full end-of-course report for every one. **Scenes 17–19** come from here — the assessor pack, the grades and the final report — because a course three weeks in has none of those yet, and a report that names TP7 on a course at TP3 is the kind of thing an assessor notices. Nothing in the film may show a finished document on the running course.

Capture from the live links, not from mock-ups. Both courses' three links are in the console; the tutor link is the one that opens everything.

---

## The three things the film must establish

1. **It lives anywhere.** Lite is a link. It opens in a browser, inside Google Classroom, inside an LMS, from a Google Drive folder — and it keeps working when the internet drops, because the pages are kept in the browser (the offline shell). Said in the first twenty seconds, shown, not just said.
2. **Who made it.** The owner is Ramy. The console says so, and every screen carries *designed and built by Ramy*. The audience should leave knowing a person made this, and which one.
3. **It's intuitive, and it's paper when you need paper.** Choose a shape and the stages appear; paste a Drive link and the worksheet is there; press Print and the whole assessor pack is a PDF. Show these as *moments*, not as features.

## Build notes before filming

- **Everything the film shows is built and live** as of 27 Sep: the owner's name on the console, the hero card, the taught criterion chips and their tip, the two demo courses, the domain. Nothing in the scene list waits on code.
- **Dictation — Chrome or Edge on a computer, always.** Safari, and every browser on iPhone and iPad, defines the engine but cannot dictate with it, so the *Dictate* button hides itself there. Film every dictation beat (scenes 8, 11, 13) in Chrome, with the microphone permitted beforehand so no permission sheet appears on screen.
- **Offline for scene 1.** The offline shell (`sw.js`) must have cached the pages once before the Wi-Fi goes off; open each page once online before filming.
- **Drive link for scene 9.** Use a real shared file; the preview iframe needs "anyone with the link".
- **Dictation window for scene 13.** Any model; the brief is what `Copy` puts on the clipboard. If it can't be shown, cut from *Copy* to *Paste something back* with the voice covering it.
- **Print dialogs.** macOS Safari's dialog is cleanest on screen; export to PDF and show the PDF in Preview for the zooms in scenes 14, 17 and 19.
