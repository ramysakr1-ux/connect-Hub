ConnectLite

For a CELTA centre

Everything your candidates write, and everything you write back

The assessed paperwork of a CELTA course in one place — plans, language analyses, self-evaluations, the four written assignments, your feedback, the grades and the reports. No accounts, no passwords, nothing to install. A course is three links: one for your tutors, one for each candidate, one for the assessor.

What it does

  • Plans, analyses and self-evaluations come in from candidates and land on the tutor's screen. No files, no email.
  • Feedback is written against the plan the candidate wrote, tagged with the CELTA 5 criteria, and returned the same day.
  • Written assignments carry your centre's own wording and criteria, and the outcome follows the marks rather than a box.
  • The assessor's pack, the Cambridge grade form and the end-of-course reports assemble themselves from what is already there.

What it is not. It is not a timetable and not a register, and it does not hold your admissions, your input sessions or your resource library. Those stay wherever they already are. Lite holds the writing — the part that has to be produced, marked, kept and shown to an assessor.

Per course, not per candidate. One price covers the whole course — up to 24 trainees, all of their tutors, and your course administrator. Nothing recurring, nothing per seat, and no charge for the assessor's access.

A course is one set of dates. You choose the start and the end while you are setting it up; from the first trainee you add, they are the course's dates — it can run a few days over, as courses do, but a new pair of dates is a new course. Your next course gets its own link.

What you should know before you decide

  • Your links work for the whole of your course. The tutors' link and each candidate's link keep working from the day you set the course up until you have finished with it — there is no session to expire and nothing to renew mid-course. The assessor's link is the one exception: it stops working fourteen days after the course end date, which is the Cambridge window.
  • Your records are not deleted on a timer. A finished course stays readable while you need it, including after the last candidate has gone, so you can print or re-print anything Cambridge asks for. When you are done with it you say so and it goes, records and uploaded materials together.
  • You can take the whole thing away as PDF. One button prints every candidate's complete record — each teaching practice with its plan, its language analysis and the candidate's self-evaluation, and every marked assignment. The grades report, the assessor's pack and the end-of-course reports each print on their own. Do that before you ask me to delete a course.
  • It keeps working without the internet. A page that has been opened once stays on the device, so a lesson plan being written when the wifi drops is not lost.
  • Where the records live. Each course's work is held in a Google Sheet and a Drive folder that I hold, and nowhere else. Candidates' materials stay on their own Drive unless they attach a file. If your centre needs the records held in your own Google account instead, say so and we will set it up that way.
  • No accounts, no passwords. A link is the way in, which is why the card each person receives says to keep it to themselves. If a tutor link gets out, your course admin can replace it in one click and every copy in circulation stops working.

I built this because I run CELTA courses myself, and the paperwork was living in five places at once — a plan in a Drive folder, feedback in a document, grades in a spreadsheet, and the assessor's pack typed out again from all three.

It does one thing: it keeps a course's writing in one place, in the words Cambridge uses, so that nothing has to be typed twice. If it would help your centre, open the tutor link above and spend five minutes in it — that will tell you more than I can.
