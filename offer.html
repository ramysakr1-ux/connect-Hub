ConnectLite For a centre

Everything your trainees write, and everything you write back

A CELTA course’s assessed paperwork in one place. No accounts, no passwords, nothing to install. A course runs on four kinds of link.

What it holds: the plans, language analyses and self-evaluations, the four written assignments, your feedback, the grades and the reports. The links are one for your tutors, one for each trainee, one for the assessor and one for each volunteer student.

What it does

  • Plans, analyses and self-evaluations come in from trainees and land on the tutor's screen. No files, no email.
  • Feedback is written against the plan the trainee wrote, tagged with the CELTA 5 criteria, and returned with one click.
  • Written assignments carry your centre's own wording and criteria, and the outcome follows the marks rather than a box.
  • The assessor's pack, the Cambridge grade form and the end-of-course reports assemble themselves from what is already there.

What you should know before you decide

  • Your links work for the whole of your course. The tutors' link and each trainee's link keep working from the day you set the course up until you have finished with it — there is no session to expire and nothing to renew mid-course. The assessor's link is the one exception: it stops at the end of the course's last day, so an assessment link is never left open indefinitely.
  • Your records are not deleted on a timer. A finished course stays readable while you need it, including after the last trainee has gone, so you can print or re-print anything Cambridge asks for. When you are done with it you say so and it goes, records and uploaded materials together.
  • You can take the whole thing away as PDF. One button prints every trainee's complete record — each teaching practice with its plan, its language analysis and the trainee's self-evaluation, and every marked assignment. The grades report, the assessor's pack and the end-of-course reports each print on their own. Do that before you ask me to delete a course.
  • It keeps working without the internet. A page that has been opened once stays on the device, so a lesson plan being written when the wifi drops is not lost.
  • No accounts, no passwords. A link is the way in, which is why the card each person receives says to keep it to themselves. If a tutor link gets out, your course admin can replace it in one click and every copy in circulation stops working.
  • A course is one set of dates. You choose the start and the end while you are setting it up; from the first trainee you add, they are the course's dates — it can run a few days over, as courses do, but a new pair of dates is a new course. Your next course gets its own link.

I built this because I run CELTA courses myself, and the paperwork was living in five places at once — a plan in a Drive folder, feedback in a document, grades in a spreadsheet, and the assessor's pack typed out again from all three.

It does one thing: it keeps a course's writing in one place, in the words Cambridge uses, so that nothing has to be typed twice. If it would help your centre, open it as a tutor below and spend five minutes in it — that will tell you more than I can.
