Handbook 9.1.2: candidates should teach on a minimum of eight occasions, within six assessed hours. A recommendation, not a requirement — the number is yours.

Handbook 3.1: a CELTA course is at least 120 contact hours between candidates and tutors, plus a minimum of 80 additional learning hours.

These two are a standing instruction in your own words, shown on the lesson plan and the self-evaluation. They are a reminder, not a lock: teaching practice moves around, and a plan that arrives late is a conversation with a tutor rather than a door that shuts. The written assignments have real deadlines — those are set on the Assignments tab.

Documents for the assessor

Handbook 14.1 asks for these before the visit. Lite already gives the assessor the portfolios, the assignment briefs and the double-marking record — paste a link for anything of yours it cannot hold, and the assessor finds it all behind their one link. Leave a box empty and it simply does not appear.

Candidates the assessor will observe

Handbook 14.2: the assessor will co-observe two candidates for one and a half hours or more of teaching practice, and at a regular assessment reviews two portfolios in full (a minimum of four at a longer two-yearly assessment). Tick the ones whose teaching they will watch and whose portfolios they will read. The pack opens with them; every other candidate stays one click below, because the assessor may read any of them.

Add candidates on the Roster tab first.

Tutor contacts

The tutors’ email addresses, for the “Email your tutor” line on the candidates’ home. For says whose tutor it is: the whole course, or one teaching practice group. A candidate sees their group’s tutor first, then the course’s. Nothing shows until an address is here.

Online rooms

Zoom, Meet or Teams links for an online or mixed-mode course, each room a button. Everyone — candidates, tutors, the assessor — sees every room and chooses theirs. Teaching practice and tutorials are suggested once per group, since each tutor holds their own. For is optional: name a teaching practice group and that group’s candidates see the room first, marked as theirs; nobody is kept out of it. The assessor sees only the rooms you tick for them — the ones they will sit in on the visit — and nothing else. Leave a link empty and it does not show.

Files for candidates

Links the candidates read: the timetable, CELTA 5, the input sessions, TP points, anything else. Each row has a switch: off, and the candidates do not see it — a cloned course arrives with every row off except the timetable. Each one becomes a line on their home screen, under the card you name — links with the same card name share a card, so “Input sessions” can hold twenty; a link with no card name is a card of its own. A row with a name and no link shows the candidates the name and “no link yet”; a bare suggestion with nothing typed is not saved.

Observation tasks

Fifteen sheets the candidates fill in: nine for their own teaching practice group, four for filmed lessons, two for live ones. The words are yours to change; the shape of each sheet is fixed, because it is what the candidate fills in and what prints.

Written assignments

Yours to rewrite: the sections, the fields, the checkboxes a candidate signs, and the criteria it is marked against. The four Cambridge assignments are the four; A5 is the centre's own.
