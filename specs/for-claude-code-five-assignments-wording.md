# Content spec: the five written assignments (default wording)

Written 22 Aug 2026, for Claude Code. This is the actual content of the 5 written
assignments as built into Connect Hub's wording builder (`8_assignment_wording.html`,
`DEFAULT_DATA`) — the wording every new centre starts with, and what should ship as the
default content when this is built into the Hub. Centres can rewrite any of it; this is
the baseline.

Each assignment has: a title, a word-count range, a format flag (continuous prose vs.
structured), marking criteria (each tied to a specific section, or general/whole-assignment
if unattached), and an ordered list of sections. Section types: **text** (an instructions
block or a prose-answer prompt), **picker** (pick N items per category — LRT only),
**fields** (a repeatable analysis block keyed to whatever was picked), **declaration**
(own-work + AI-use, present on every assignment, always re-taken fresh on a resubmission
round).

---

## 1. LRT — Language Related Tasks
**750–1,000 words · structured**

**Criteria:** Identifies meaning, form and pronunciation accurately for each item (tied to
"Analyse each item"). Uses correct terminology throughout (same section).

- **Before you start:** You'll pick 2 items from Category A and 2 from Category B, then
  analyse each one — meaning, form, pronunciation and so on. Work through this one step at
  a time; nothing is submitted until the last step.
- **Pick your items** (Category A: Grammar item 1/2/3 · Category B: Lexis item 1/2/3 · pick
  2 from each — centre replaces these placeholder item names with real ones).
- **Analyse each item** (repeat for each item picked): Meaning ("The essential meaning of
  the item, in this context"); Clarification of meaning ("CCQs (with answers), and/or a
  timeline, diagram, or personalised example"); Form ("Break down each part. Use accurate,
  specific terminology"); Pronunciation ("Phonemic script — weak forms, stress, linking,
  problem sounds"); Appropriacy ("Only where relevant — register, more/less formal
  alternatives"); Anticipated problems & solutions ("Cover meaning, form, AND pronunciation
  — a solution for each problem you raise").
- **Declaration:** "This is my own work." / "All sources are referenced, in the format
  (Author, Year, p. ##)." + AI-use toggle.

## 2. LSRT — Language Skills Related Task
**750–1,000 words · structured**

**Criteria:** Identifies sub-skills and task types accurately (tied to "Sub-skills and task
types"). Anticipates problems with the skill and proposes workable solutions (tied to
"Anticipated problems and solutions").

- **The material:** The lesson or extract you are analysing — not the whole unit, just the
  skills-focused part.
- **Sub-skills and task types:** Name the specific sub-skills the material targets (e.g.
  skimming for gist, listening for specific information, turn-taking, planning before
  writing) and how each task exercises them.
- **How the lesson stages the skill:** Lead-in, pre-task (vocabulary/prediction), the task
  itself, and post-task — what each stage is for, and how it prepares learners for the
  next.
- **Anticipated problems and solutions:** Problems with the skill itself (not language) —
  task difficulty, background knowledge, text length, unfamiliar text types — and how you
  would address each.
- **Declaration:** same as LRT.

## 3. FOL — Focus on the Learner
**750–1,000 words · continuous prose**

**Criteria:** Names the grammar and pronunciation problems accurately, using correct
terminology (general). Proposes activities that genuinely address the stated problems
(general).

- **Before you start:** You'll describe your current TP group, then identify one grammar
  and one pronunciation difficulty they've shown — two examples of each — and present one
  activity for each problem. Word count: 750–1,000 words of continuous prose, not counting
  the bibliography or appendices.
- **A — Your TP group:** Describe your group of learners, covering group size, gender, age
  range and level.
- **B — The grammar problem:** Name the grammar area you've chosen to focus on, using
  correct terminology, with two examples.
- **C — The pronunciation problem:** Name the pronunciation area you've chosen to focus
  on, using correct terminology and IPA script, with two examples.
- **D — The grammar task:** Attach one task in Appendix 1 that addresses the grammar
  problem above — just one, not a choice of options.
- **E — The pronunciation task:** Attach one task in Appendix 2 that addresses the
  pronunciation problem above. Repeating after the teacher or drilling doesn't count as a
  task.
- **Declaration:** same as LRT.

## 4. LFC — Lessons from the Classroom
**750–1,000 words · continuous prose**

**Criteria:** Reflects honestly on strengths and action points with reference to TP
feedback (general). Draws specific, actionable insights from observation of others
(general).

- **Before you start:** This assignment is a reflection on your progress during the
  course. You'll summarise your main teaching strengths and action points, insights from
  observing peers and experienced teachers, and how you'll continue developing after the
  CELTA. Word count: 750–1,000 words of continuous prose, not counting the bibliography or
  appendices.
- **A — Identifying strengths:** Browse your 3 most recent TPs' feedback and identify 3
  teaching strengths you've shown.
- **B — Identifying action points:** Browse your 3 most recent TPs' feedback and identify
  3 action points you still have.
- **C — Reflecting on observation of others:** For each action point from Part B, give
  one example of how another teacher addressed it — through peer observation, a tutor's
  live observation, or a video observation.
- **D — Post-course development:** Choose 3 areas you want to focus on after the course.
- **Declaration:** same as LRT.

## 5. Assignment 5 — Plagiarism Reflection (centre sanction)
**750–1,000 words · not one of the four Cambridge assignments — no word-min/max or format
flag stored, since it's centre-issued, not Cambridge-numbered**

Set only after an upheld malpractice case. Does not count toward the three-of-four rule
and cannot raise or lower the certificate grade. One chance, pass or fail — same as the
resubmission it accompanies.

- **Before you start:** This is not a research essay about plagiarism. It is an account of
  what happened in your own case, which rule it breached, and what you will do
  differently. Write it in your own words — a general essay on academic honesty will not
  pass, and will be scanned like everything else. 750–1,000 words, due by the last day of
  the course.
- **What this does and does not affect:** It does not count toward the three-of-four rule,
  and it cannot raise or lower your certificate grade. One chance, pass or fail — the same
  as the resubmission it accompanies. It is a condition set by the centre, not a Cambridge
  assignment, and it is not numbered with the four. It stays with the case in your file. An
  assessor asking how the centre handled this sees both the decision and what followed.
- **1 — What happened (~200 words):** In your own words: what you submitted, and how it
  came about. An account, not an apology. What you were doing, when, and what you were
  thinking. If you disagree with the finding, say so here — that is allowed, and this
  section is where it goes.
- **2 — Which rule it breached (~200 words):** Quote the clause from the centre's
  malpractice policy that applies, and say why it applies to what you did. Quote the
  relevant line from the Cambridge AI guidance and the candidate agreement you accepted
  when you set up your account. Both documents are in the resource hub. You have already
  signed one of them.
- **3 — Why it matters here (~150 words):** What it would mean for a learner, a colleague
  or a centre if a teacher's materials or claims were not their own. Not why plagiarism is
  wrong in general — why it matters for someone about to teach.
- **4 — What you will do differently (~250 words):** How you will note sources while you
  read, so this cannot happen by accident. How you will use AI tools and declare them,
  specifically. What you will do at 1am with a deadline in seven hours and nothing
  written. That is the situation this is really about.
- **Declaration:** same as LRT.

## Notes for implementation

- Every section's exact copy above should ship verbatim as the default — centres can edit
  or replace any of it (including re-importing a whole assignment from a file), but this
  is the starting content, not placeholder text to improve on.
- LRT's picker category items ("Grammar item 1/2/3", "Lexis item 1/2/3") ARE placeholders —
  every centre is expected to replace these with real items before running the course.
  Everything else is real, final course wording.
- The declaration block's exact text ("This is my own work." / "All sources are
  referenced, in the format (Author, Year, p. ##).") plus the AI-use toggle is identical
  across all 5 assignments — implement once, reuse everywhere.
- Full mechanism (per-field criteria linking, resubmission lock/carry-forward, outcome
  derivation) is documented separately in
  `for-claude-code-assignment-compliance-audit.md` (v4) — this file is content only.
