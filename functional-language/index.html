Answer Key — Exponent 1 Reveal
“Can you pass me the sugar, please?”
Form
can + subject pronoun + infinitive verb (no ‘to’) + object pronoun + definite article + noun — present simple
Function
A polite request
Phonology
/kən/ weak form; /kənju/ linking of ‘can’ + ‘you’
CCQs
Is it past, present or future? (Present) · Does she have the sugar? (No) · Does she want the sugar? (Yes)
Context
Two friends are drinking tea in a café. The table is a bit long and the other friend can't reach the sugar, so they need to ask for it.

Answer Key — Exponent 2 Reveal
“You should stay in bed.”
Form
subject pronoun + should + infinitive verb (no ‘to’) + prepositional phrase (in + noun) — modal verb, present
Function
Giving advice
Phonology
‘should’ weak form /ʃəd/; sentence stress on ‘stay’ and ‘bed’; falling intonation
CCQs
Is the person ill? (Yes) · Is this an order or a suggestion? (Suggestion) · Does the speaker think it’s a good idea? (Yes) · Must they stay in bed? (No)
Context
A friend has a bad cold but is thinking of going to work anyway. Their flatmate tells them it’s better to rest.
answer key
Answer Key — Exponent 3 Reveal
“If only I'd been more romantic with her.”
Form
If only + subject pronoun + had (‘d) + past participle + comparative adjective + prepositional phrase — past perfect
Function
Expressing regret about the past
Phonology
‘I’d’ contracted /aɪd/; ‘been’ weak form /bɪn/; stress on ‘only’ and ‘romantic’; falling intonation
CCQs
Is he talking about the past? (Yes) · Was he romantic with her? (No) · Can he change it now? (No) · Does he feel sorry about it? (Yes)
Context
A man is telling a friend about his ex-girlfriend, who has just left him. He’s thinking about what he could have done differently.
