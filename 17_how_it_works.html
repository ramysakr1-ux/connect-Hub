How Connect Lite works

A whole CELTA course runs on it, for five kinds of person, with no accounts and no passwords. Your link is your way in — that is the only thing you have to keep.

Start here

Three things that are true of every screen.

  1. The link is the key. There are no usernames and no passwords. Whoever holds the link can open the page, which is why your own link is yours and is not for forwarding. Keep the whole thing, including everything after the ?.
  2. It saves as you type. You do not press Save to keep your work. Nothing you write is lost by closing the tab, and nothing reaches anybody else until you turn it in or return it.
  3. Turning in is the moment that matters. Before you turn in, a form is yours alone. After you turn it in, it is your tutor's to read and you can no longer change it.
The screens work without a signal. What you write while offline is kept and catches up with the course record when you are back online.

If you are a trainee

Four rooms, in the order you need them, and one grid that shows where you are.

Before you teach

Your TP points page holds what you are teaching: the aim, the slot, the level, and the pages and audio your tutor attached, with the other two lessons of the day beside yours for reference. It appears when your tutor releases it, and its date comes from the timetable.

Write your lesson plan and language analysis in one place. Choose a lesson shape and the stages fill themselves in; change any of them. Turn it in before you teach. Under the materials, a switch shares a file with the volunteer students for that lesson, so they can look at it before they come.

After you teach, before you read the feedback

Fill in your self-evaluation. Your own plan sits beside it so you can see what you said you would do. Writing it before you read your tutor's comments is the point of it — it is your judgement first, not a reply to theirs.

When it comes back

Your teaching practice record holds every feedback your tutors have returned, with their comments on your plan and your self-evaluation. It stays there all course, and the whole lot prints as one PDF at the end.

Alongside all of it

The written assignments, in your centre's own wording, each showing the criteria it is marked against. You can resubmit where your centre allows it, and the record shows where each one stands.

Your progress puts every teaching practice and every assignment on one grid. It is the same view your tutor has of you — there is nothing about you they can see here that you cannot.

Around the teaching

The timetable is the course's own and it is alive: today is marked, each session carries the online room's Join link, and the deadlines it shows are the same ones your assignments show. In the last week the TP planning grid is yours: your group plans TP7 and TP8 together, each of you editing your own row.

Your home carries the course noticeboard, where tutors post announcements, with a line to email your tutor beneath it and the centre's hours. The links the centre has shared sit there too, next to your own private ones. The six observation tasks have their own page, and your CELTA 5 is built in: its tables fill from the record and you sign it on screen.

If you are a tutor

One link opens the course. Put your name in once and everything you return is signed with it.

The dashboard

Who has turned in what, and what is waiting for you. Open a trainee from there. The top row is the daily things: the noticeboard, the timetable, the TP points, the planning grid, the volunteer register.

The timetable and the TP points

Build the timetable on screen, paste one in from a sheet, or choose the Excel file; set the day shape once and every day follows it. Rooms and the course clock come from Course admin, so a trainee in another time zone sees both times. Move a deadline on the timetable and the assignment moves with it.

TP points rotate by themselves: the seven aims spread across the course, the teaching order turns each day, and a lesson that was not to standard is followed by another of the same family. Attach book pages and an audio link to each point, then release the day to its group; every trainee sees their own lesson and the other two for reference.

The volunteer students

The volunteer register takes the names one at a time, pasted, or from an Excel file, and is one row per student and one tap per teaching day: in the room, or there for part of it. Hours come from the timetable's own lessons and a class counts when they were there for most of it. Copy their link gives each student their own page, with the next class, the Join button, and anything the trainees have shared. The certificate of attendance prints from the register, or from their page once they reach the centre's hours. The register shows who has agreed to the centre's joining note.

Returning feedback

Write one point per line under strengths and action points, for planning and for teaching, and tag each point with the criteria it relates to. Star an action point and it carries into that trainee's next teaching practice as a priority. Their plan and self-evaluation are pulled in beside you as you write.

Marking assignments

Mark against the criteria, record the outcome, and set a resubmission where one is needed. The trainee sees the marked record, not a grade on its own.

At the end of the course

The grades report is the Cambridge centre grade form: the same fields, in the same order, in Cambridge's own wording, so it goes into Appian by paste rather than by retyping. Under each section, Add from the TP records hands back the points you already wrote about that trainee in their teaching practice feedback, newest first — click one and it is in the box, with its criterion code.

The assessor pack gathers the timetable, the portfolios, the grades and the registers behind one view-only link to send. The link stops working when the course ends.

If you run the centre

Course admin sets up the course; the owner console mints them.

Set the course name, dates and number of teaching practices, add your trainees, and send each of them their own invitation. Write your own version of the four assignments — rewrite the instructions, add or remove sections, set the deadlines — and every trainee on the course sees your wording, not ours.

Course admin also holds the online rooms, the course's time zone, the Google Classroom and other links you share, the observation-task wording, the tutor contacts, and the hours a volunteer needs for a certificate. When the course is over, start the next course from this one carries the wording, the rooms and the timetable shape over, with nothing switched on until you say so.

Everything the course produces lives in one Google Sheet that belongs to you. If Connect Lite went away tomorrow, the record would still be yours.

If you are the assessor

One link, read-only, nothing to sign into.

The pack holds the assessment timetable and the joining link, the trainee portfolios and descriptions, the provisional and final grades, the tracker and marking record, and the attendance registers, including the volunteer students'. Nothing on it can be changed from your link, and the centre can see that too. The link works until the course ends.

Questions

The ones people actually ask.

Do I need an account or a password?

No. The link you were sent is your way in. There is nothing to set up and nothing to remember.

I have lost my link.

Ask your tutor, or your centre, to send it again. Nobody can look it up for you from an email address, because there are no accounts to look up.

Where has my work gone?

Work in progress is kept in the browser you wrote it in. If you started a plan on your laptop, it is on your laptop — it does not appear on your phone until you turn it in. Turned-in work is on the course record and follows you anywhere.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes — every screen works at phone width. A few things are simply wider than a phone, like the plan table and the tracker grid; those scroll sideways inside their own box, and the page itself never does.

What if I close the tab by mistake?

Nothing is lost. Every form saves as you type. Open your link again and carry on where you were.

Who can see what I have written?

Nobody, until you turn it in. After that, your tutors on the course can see it, and the assessor can see it at the end. Other trainees never can.

Can I change something after turning it in?

No — that is what turning in means. Tell your tutor; they can return it to you if there is a reason to.

Two of us tutor this course. Do we each get a link?

A course has one tutor link and you both use it. Each of you puts your own name in on your own device, and everything you return is signed with the name in front of you. You see each other's work; neither of you overwrites the other.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once you have opened a screen, it opens again without a signal, and what you write is kept until you are back online.

I am a volunteer student. Can I read this in my language?

The centre's joining note and the section above are in Turkish, Arabic, Russian, Persian, Ukrainian and English. Your page itself stays in English — it is an English class — and it keeps to a few short lines.

Is this the same as Connect?

No. Connect Lite runs a course; Connect runs a centre. If you get on with this, Connect is the bigger version of the same ideas.
