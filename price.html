The rules, all of them

  • A course with one teaching practice group counts as half a course. Six trainees or fewer is one group, so on the ten-course rate that course costs £110. A small cohort is never the expensive way to run one.
  • Courses you have bought do not expire. A block can be spent over as many years as it takes. Nothing lapses and nothing renews.
  • Per course, not per trainee. One price covers the whole course — up to 24 trainees, all of their tutors, your course administrator, and the assessor’s access at no charge.
  • A course is a course. Four weeks full-time, five weeks, or part-time across three months — the length and the shape make no difference to the price. What a course is: one set of dates and one cohort. You choose the start and the end while you are setting it up, and it can run a few days over, as courses do, but a new pair of dates is a new course.
  • Volunteer students are not counted. However many come, their register, their own pages in six languages, the day-before reminders and the signed certificates are all in the price. Nobody pays per student, and the people who come in to be taught cost a centre nothing to look after.
  • Nothing recurring. No subscription, no seat fees, no charge for the volunteer students’ pages or for printing anything.

I kept this as simple as I could because I have been the person on the other side of a quote with four tiers and a footnote. One number, one unit, and a smaller number if your course is small.

If your centre needs something this page does not cover — an invoice in another currency, a purchase order, a first course to try before you commit — just ask. None of that is a problem.
