Connected speech

Five features, five activities, then the full picture: elision, assimilation, intrusive sounds, linking, and weak forms.

Can you read these? Lead-in7 min

Try reading these out loud. They look strange written down, but say them fast and listen for the real sentence hiding inside. Click each one to reveal it.

activity 1
Elision trivia Guess the fact8 min

Here's the same phrase written two ways: how it "should" sound, and how it actually sounds when a sound gets dropped. Read the second version out loud, work out the phrase, then guess the real-world fact.

activity 2
The dictation computer Puzzle10 min

A dictation computer prints exactly what it hears, and connected speech confuses it. Each sentence on the left has a mistake. Match it to the response on the right that gives you a clue to the real sentence, then say what the computer should have printed.

Each printed sentence has exactly one reply that gives the game away. Match it, and the real sentence appears with the feature that caused the mis-hearing.

activity 3
The link maze Escape the grid12 min

Your phrase is waiting in the dark start cell. Say it out loud: which sound joins the two words, /j/, /w/ or /r/? That tells you which way to click. Land on a new cell, say its phrase, and move again. Reach the red finish cell to win.

/j/ moves left /w/ moves up /r/ moves right
activity 4
Mark the links Sherlock Holmes8 min

Click between two words to mark a link (just like drawing a ◡ shape by hand). Read it aloud smoothly, gliding over each link you've marked.

Your marks show in gold. Suggested links, when revealed, show in teal.

activity 5
Spot the weak forms Click the words8 min

Say each sentence at natural speed. Click the word or words you think get "swallowed": weakened, so the listener mainly hears the content words. Then reveal to check.

The key

Lead-in: I hear you want to meet our son; He knows four people who wear their old clothes to work; Our jumper isn’t made yet; We’ve seen our glasses; My knees are quite all right; Her sister has got a tortoise. The dictation computer’s ten answers are on the page after each match.

The maze route, from the start cell: Draw a line (/r/) right to We saw a film (/r/) right to Two or three (/w/) up to A queue of people (/w/) up to Hello everybody (/w/) up to Three and a half (/j/) left to Tea or coffee (/j/) left to I agree (/j/) left to Give me a ring (/j/) left to Go ahead (/w/) up to Never again (/r/) right to Score a goal (/r/) right to Finish.

Watch for

  • Reading the phonemic script letter by letter. The sound comes first; the script is a record of it.
  • The maze done by trial and error. Make them say the phrase out loud before every click; that is the activity.

Sources. The dictation-computer sentences and the Sherlock Holmes linking text are classic CELTA input material (the computer puzzle follows Mark Hancock, Pronunciation Games, Cambridge University Press, 1995); the link maze and the elision trivia are the tutor's own.
