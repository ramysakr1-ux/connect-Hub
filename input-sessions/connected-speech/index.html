activity 1
Elision trivia Guess the fact

Here's the same phrase written two ways: how it "should" sound, and how it actually sounds when a sound gets dropped. Read the second version out loud, work out the phrase, then guess the real-world fact.

activity 2
The dictation computer Puzzle

A dictation computer prints exactly what it hears, and connected speech confuses it. Each sentence on the left has a mistake. Match it to the response on the right that gives you a clue to the real sentence, then say what the computer should have printed.

Each printed sentence has exactly one reply that gives the game away. Match it, and the real sentence appears with the feature that caused the mis-hearing.

activity 3
The link maze Find your way out

You start in the middle, facing up. Say the phrase in the cell in front of you: the sound that joins its two words tells you which way to go next. Keep going until you leave the maze. Which letter do you come out at? Tap any cell to hear its phrase.

/j/ turn left /w/ go straight on /r/ turn right

activity 4
Mark the links A short story

Click between two words to mark a link (just like drawing a ◡ shape by hand). Read it aloud smoothly, gliding over each link you've marked.

Your marks show in gold. Suggested links, when revealed, show in teal.

activity 5
Spot the weak forms Click the words

Say each sentence at natural speed. Click the word or words you think get "swallowed": weakened, so the listener mainly hears the content words. Then reveal to check.

Dictogloss Listen and rebuild

Your tutor will read a short text twice, at normal speed. The first time, only listen. The second time, write down every word you catch. Then, in pairs, rebuild the text so that it makes sense and is correct. It does not have to be word for word. Keep the text covered until you have finished.

Then look at the text. Where did you miss words, and what was happening to them in the speech?

The text, and what happened to it

I’d have gone out last night, but my mum rang just as I was leaving. She’d lost her keys and couldn’t get into the flat. So I picked up a pizza, drove over, and spent half the evening looking for them. They were in the fridge, next to the milk.

The question for the room

Which of your missing words were weak forms, which were joined and which were dropped? What would you do with this text in a lesson?
