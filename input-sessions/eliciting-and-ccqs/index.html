The key

The practice table’s meanings and CCQs are on the page behind Reveal answer; they follow Workman. The practice words in Your turn have no key: any technique that gets the word out of a student is right, and any CCQ with a yes-or-no or short answer that does not contain the word is right.

Watch for

  • CCQs that contain the target language ("Does she use to smoke?"). Send them back to meaning.
  • "Do you understand?" dressed up as a CCQ. A CCQ has an answer you can hear.
  • Eliciting that becomes a guessing game with no context. The scenario does the work; without one, the students are guessing the teacher’s mind.
  • Time. The practice table runs over if every pair does all seven. Three sentences each, then share.

Sources. The practice table's seven sentences, meanings and CCQs follow Graham Workman, Concept Questions and Time Lines (Gem Publishing, 2005). The eleven ways to elicit and the practice words are the tutor's own.
