Text-Based Presentation of Language

The main aim will usually be: To clarify and provide controlled practice of ______.

The language is introduced using a reading or listening briefly first (but this is not the main aim), and then language from the text is clarified (checking meaning, form and pronunciation) before doing further practice.

StageWhat it is for
Lead-in / building contextTo generate interest in the topic, theme or context of the text or listening.
Reading or listening taskTo practice reading or listening for ______. To introduce the target language via a text or listening.
Highlighting target languageTo highlight the target language by use of eliciting, a guiding task or an underlining activity.
Clarifying target languageTo clarify the meaning of the target language. To model and provide controlled practice of pronunciation. To highlight form.
Language practiceTo provide controlled, less controlled or freer oral or written practice of ______.
FeedbackTo establish correct answers, or to deal with the results of the task.

You may need to briefly pre-teach some vocabulary before the reading task.

Language Practice

The main aim will usually be: To provide controlled or freer practice of ______.

This is connected to, and follows on from, a previous language lesson. The language is what another teacher has taught, so your lesson follows on from the previous teacher, who will have clarified it. It involves different practice stages, for example controlled written practice, controlled oral practice, less controlled written or speaking practice, and freer written or oral practice.

You may not need a lead-in, but if you do, remember that this lesson is the second part of the previous lesson and, if possible, keep the same context. This type of lesson may need some student preparation, for example pre-teaching, controlled practice, group discussion or role preparation. Feedback stages are also important.

StageWhat it is for
Lead-in (optional)See the notes above.
Set upTo introduce and set up the practice activity. To highlight the target language to be used in the activity.
Controlled practice activityTo provide controlled or less controlled written or oral practice of ______, depending on your lesson's aims. The teacher monitors as students do the activity. Hot error correction could be provided.
Freer practice activityTo provide less controlled or freer oral or written practice of ______. The teacher monitors as students do the activity.
Feedback and error correctionTo establish correct answers, or to deal with the results of the task. To provide feedback on use of the target language, dealing with errors and problems.

The above is repeated with each controlled or freer practice activity. You may need to pre-teach some items.

Test-Teach-Test Presentation of Language

The main aim will usually be: To clarify and provide controlled practice of ______.

The students do an exercise at the start using the target language with no help from the teacher (a diagnostic test). The teacher monitors the task very carefully to see what problems the students have, and then clarifies (checking meaning, form and pronunciation) as necessary: any new language or items the students had problems with. For example, if there are 15 pieces of vocabulary in the test, the teacher would not clarify every item. This is followed by further practice (the second test).

StageWhat it is for
Lead-inTo generate interest in the topic or theme of the lesson.
First test (diagnostic)To test students' current understanding and identify gaps in vocabulary related to ______.
Teach (clarifying)To deal with the meaning, pronunciation and form of vocabulary related to ______, with emphasis on the items students did not know or were confused about.
Second test (controlled practice)To provide controlled or less controlled written or oral practice of ______.
(Freer practice)To provide less controlled or freer oral or written practice of ______.
FeedbackTo establish correct answers, or deal with the results of the task. To provide feedback on use of the target language, dealing with errors and problems.

There might not be time for the second test in a 45-minute lesson. In that case the next teacher will do it as their lesson (language practice).

PracticeTo consolidate the key concepts of the target language. To provide a focus on accuracy within the context of ______.
ProductionTo provide an authentic activity in which the target language is naturally generated. To provide a focus on fluency within the context of ______.

Receptive Skills: a listening or reading lesson

The main aim will usually be: For students to practice reading or listening for gist and detail comprehension (for example).

The lesson can be divided into pre-, while- and post-reading or listening stages. Choose the stages you need.

Productive Skills: speaking or writing

The main aim will usually be: To practice oral or written fluency in relation to ______ (topic).

The lesson can usually be divided into preparation for speaking or writing, and the speaking or writing itself. Choose the stages you need.

StageWhat it is for
Lead-inTo activate students' existing knowledge of the topic. To generate interest in the topic or theme of the lesson.
Preparing to write or speakTo generate or provide ideas. To give students an opportunity to brainstorm ideas, prepare notes and think about what they will say in the task. (This may involve students reading or listening to something similar to what they will produce themselves: to provide them with a model for the task.)
(Useful language)To provide and clarify language students may find useful for completing the writing or speaking task. This is not target language. It is there to help them perform the task, that's all: students do not have to use it.
Speaking or writing taskTo develop oral fluency through a ______ task. To develop writing skills in relation to writing ______.
Feedback and error correction on the oral or written taskTo provide quick feedback on the content, results or outcomes, and then to deal with generic errors.

It might be necessary to have a pre-teaching stage during this lesson.

Controlled practice

  • It should force students to consider aspects of meaning and form.
  • It should be staged before freer practice, to avoid fossilization of errors.
  • Recognition is not a legitimate practice task, because it gives the teacher no evidence of the learners' ability to use the target language. For example, underlining examples of the past simple in a text is recognition, not practice.
  • You must adapt material to make sure restricted practice is within the theme of the lesson.
  • Creating a short dialogue is one way to establish a context and a relationship between speakers.

Is the focus of the task on meaning (use), on form (syntax), or on both? The example target language here is the difference between used to and would.

Task typeExampleMeaning, form or both?

A meaning-and-form task like these can also be used for peer teaching and guided discovery.
