Slang and idioms

Trainer notes
Task 1

Do you speak slang?

Talk with a partner.

For the trainees watching The lead-in makes slang personal and shows the class it exists in every language.
  1. Do you use slang in your own language? With whom?
  2. Who uses the most slang in your country? Why?
  3. Do you know any English slang? Where did you learn it?
Task 2

Two kinds of sick

Read the two dialogues. What does sick mean in each? Is it positive or negative? Who are the speakers?

For the trainees watching Context before words: the same word, two meanings. They work it out from who is speaking and where.
1

A: What seems to be the problem?

B: I've felt sick since last night.

A: Did you eat anything unusual?

B: Some fish that smelled a bit strange.

2

A: Did you see the game last night?

B: No! Did we win?

A: Four–one. And the last goal was sick.

B: No way!

In dialogue 1, sick means…
In dialogue 2, sick means…
Task 3

Seven slang words

Guess, then match each word to its meaning.

For the trainees watching Students guess first, then match. Guessing makes them think about the context, which is the skill.
1. wicked
2. wrecked
3. chill
4. stan
5. fit
6. lowkey
7. mint
  1. extremely tired (or very drunk)
  2. attractive (British)
  3. excellent, really good
  4. perfect; in excellent condition
  5. calm down; relax
  6. a little, or secretly
  7. to be a huge fan of someone
Task 4

Use the slang

Complete each sentence with a word from Task 3.

For the trainees watching Controlled practice: the right word in the right sentence. It checks meaning and form at once.
1. Calm down, it's only a game. Just _____ out!
2. I danced until four in the morning, so I'm totally _____ today.
3. My sister has posters of the band all over her room. She really _____s them.
4. I'm _____ worried about the exam, but don't tell anyone.
5. Have you heard their new song? It's _____!
6. He's been going to the gym every day. He's looking really _____.
7. My grandad's old bike is still in _____ condition.
Task 5

Six idioms

Read the sentences. Match each idiom in bold to its meaning.

For the trainees watching Idioms are taught in a sentence, never alone. The sentence carries the meaning.
1. The tickets sold out in an hour. If you didn't buy one on Monday, you missed the boat.
2. Failing that exam was a blessing in disguise: I found a course I really love.
3. It's his first day here. Cut him some slack.
4. The party got out of hand when two hundred people turned up.
5. To make a long story short, we missed the plane.
6. Working from home two days a week gives me the best of both worlds.
  1. to lose a chance because you were too late
  2. something bad that turns out to be good
  3. to be less hard on someone
  4. to become impossible to control
  5. to say it quickly, without all the details
  6. the good parts of two different things
Task 6

Where would you say it?

In pairs: would you use each word or idiom with friends, at work, or both? Tap to change its color.

For the trainees watching Register matters as much as meaning: where can you say it? I let them disagree.
With friendsBothAt work
Task 7

Ask and answer

Talk with a partner.

For the trainees watching Personal questions with the new language, so it becomes theirs.
  1. When did something turn out to be a blessing in disguise for you?
  2. When did you last miss the boat?
  3. Is there anyone you stan? Why?
  4. When should people cut their teachers some slack?
  5. What does the way you speak say about you?
Task 8

Guess the word

In groups: choose a word or idiom from the lesson. Explain it without saying it. The others guess.

For the trainees watching A game that recycles everything: explaining a word is good speaking practice too.
Task 9

Role play

In pairs, choose a situation. Use as many words and idioms from the lesson as you can.

For the trainees watching The freest stage: a role play where the slang fits. I note errors and good uses for feedback.
  1. Two friends in their twenties talk about their favourite bands and shows.
  2. Two colleagues discuss a rumour that two people from the office are dating.
