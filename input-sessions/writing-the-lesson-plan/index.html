Fix this plan

A trainee wrote this for a B1 vocabulary lesson. It breaks at least six of the things you have looked at today. In pairs, find each problem and rewrite the line.

Main aim: To teach vocabulary. Personal aim: To be more confident. Lead-in · 5 min · T–S: The teacher will show some pictures and the students will talk about them. Clarification · 15 min: Teach the words. CCQs. Practice · 10 min · PW / GW / OC: Gap-fill. Anticipated problem (on the lesson plan): Students may not know how to pronounce “cupboard”. Solution: Help them.

Six problems, and one way to fix each

1. The main aim names nothing. Which words, and in what context? “To clarify and practice eight words for kitchen objects (kettle, cupboard, sink…) in the context of moving into a new flat.”

2. The personal aim can’t be checked, and doesn’t come from an action point. “To check my instructions with an ICQ before I hand out the gap-fill (TP2 action point).”

3. The lead-in is prose about the teacher, not instructions to follow. “Show four photos of kitchens. Ss in pairs: which kitchen would you like, and why? 2 min. OCFB: take two answers.”

4. “Teach the words. CCQs.” is a summary. Write out which words, how each one is clarified, and the CCQs themselves.

5. Three interaction patterns for one stage, and no instructions. “Ss do the gap-fill alone (Ind), check in pairs (PW). OCFB.” One pattern per step, honestly recorded.

6. The problem is on the wrong sheet, and “help them” is not a solution. Pronunciation goes on the Language Analysis sheet, with the stress and the sounds marked. A lesson-plan problem would be: “Ss finish the gap-fill at different times. Solution: early finishers write two sentences of their own with the words.”

The checklist to keep

Aims: name the skill or the language exactly; always give the context; vocabulary aims list the items; personal aims come from your last action points. Procedure: bullet points and imperative verbs; write the instruction out; clear who does what, and for how long; a colleague could teach from it. Interaction: one pattern per stage, honestly recorded (Ind · PW · GW · T–S · S–S · OC · OCFB). Anticipated problems: management, tasks, tech and timing on the plan; meaning, form and pronunciation on the LA sheet; every problem gets a real solution.

Your turn: write two stages

A page from a made-up coursebook. Plan the first two stages of a reading lesson from it, a lead-in and a gist task, the way you would on your plan. Then swap with another pair and check theirs against today’s four decisions.

Unit 4 · Night work (B1) A magazine article, about 350 words: “The baker who never sees the sun”, about a man who starts work at eleven at night in a city bakery. A photo of him at work, the street dark outside. Exercise 1. Would you like to work at night? Why, or why not? Exercise 2. Read the article quickly. Which sentence is the best summary? (three options)

One way to write it

Main aim: To give practice in reading for gist and for specific information, in the context of a magazine article about a baker who works at night.

Lead-in · to raise interest in the topic and get ideas about night work · 5 min
• Show the photo. Ask: “What time is it? What’s his job?” (T–S)
• Ss in pairs: would you like to work at night? Why, or why not? 2 min. (PW)
• Take two or three answers. (OCFB)

Gist · to give practice in reading for gist · 5 min
• Instruction: “Read the article quickly. Which sentence is the best summary? You have two minutes.”
• ICQs: “Do you read every word?” (No.) “How long have you got?” (Two minutes.)
• Ss read alone, 2 min. (Ind) Check in pairs, 1 min. (PW)
• Nominate one pair: which summary, and why? (OCFB)

Check theirs: is the aim exact, with a context? Bullet points and imperatives? Could a colleague teach it? One pattern per step?

One Language Analysis item

The Language Analysis sheet is where meaning, form and pronunciation go. Fill in one item of it, in pairs, in eight minutes, for this sentence from a B1 lesson: She’s been living in Madrid for three years.

One way to fill it in

Meaning. Elena moved to Madrid three years ago and still lives there. CCQs: Did she move to Madrid? (Yes, three years ago.) Does she live there now? (Yes.) Is the three years finished? (No.)

Form. She’s been living | in Madrid | for three years. Subject + has / have + been + verb with -ing + for or since, then the time.

Pronunciation. /ʃiːz bɪn ˈlɪvɪŋ/: the ’s is contracted and been is weak, /bɪn/. The stress falls on LIVing, MaDRID and THREE YEARS. The voice falls at the end.

Anticipated problems. (1) Learners say She has living, dropping been. Solution: board the pattern in chunks with been underlined, and drill the whole chunk. (2) for and since: for goes with a length of time, since with a starting point. Solution: two timelines. (3) been said as /biːn/. Solution: model it weak, in the chunk, not alone.

The check

Is each of the four boxes about meaning, form or pronunciation only? Does a problem about the task or the room appear here? It belongs on the lesson plan.
