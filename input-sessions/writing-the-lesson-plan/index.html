Why plan at all?

In pairs, two minutes. Why do we write a lesson plan? Note as many reasons as you can.

  1. To know exactly what the learners will be able to do by the end: clear aims.
  2. So every stage has a reason and leads into the next one.
  3. To fit the lesson into the time, and know what to cut if you run late.
  4. To meet the problems before the learners do, with a solution ready.
  5. To plan your words: the instructions, ICQs and CCQs written out, not made up on the spot.
  6. To have the materials, the copies and the board ready before you walk in.
  7. So someone else could teach from it. Your tutor reads it while watching you.
  8. And because you have to: planning is on the course, and Cambridge's criteria 4a to 4n are all about it. As they say: fail to plan, plan to…

The rest of the plan

Aims, procedure, interaction and problems are the four big decisions. A plan your tutor can watch you teach from needs a few more things. In pairs, check this plan against the list and find what is missing.

Class profileTiming (4h)Order and flow (4b)Variety and balance (4g)Materials (4c, 4d)Instructions and checksBoard and correction
B1 reading lesson, 45 minutes Class profile: Nice group. Materials: Handout. Lead-in · 5 min · PW: Ss discuss the photos. Gist · PW: Ss read the text. Detail · 20 min · Ind, PW: Ss answer questions 1 to 8 and check in pairs. Speaking · 15 min · GW: Discuss.

Seven things missing, and one way to fix each

1. Class profile. “Nice group” tells your tutor nothing. “12 adults, B1, mostly Turkish L1. Confident speakers, slower readers. They met the topic in TP2.”

2. Timing. The gist stage has no time, and the stages leave nothing for feedback. Give every stage a time that adds up to 45, and say what goes if you run late: “If short of time, cut the speaking to 8 minutes.”

3. Order and flow. “Ss read the text” is not a gist task, and “Discuss” does not grow out of the text. “Gist: which title fits the text best? 2 min.” “Speaking: would you live like the people in the text? Why?”

4. Variety and balance. Every stage is pairs or groups, and there is no open-class feedback anywhere, so nobody checks the answers. Add “OCFB: nominate for answers, board the tricky ones” after each task.

5. Materials. Which handout? “Student's Book p. 50, ex. 2 and 3. 12 copies and 2 spare.” Anything you made or took from elsewhere says where it came from.

6. Instructions and checks. No instruction is written out, there is no ICQ, and nobody knows who works with whom. “Read alone and choose the title. You have two minutes. Are you writing? (No.) How long? (Two minutes.) Then check with the person on your right.”

7. Board and correction. Nothing is planned for the board, and no correction is planned for the speaking. “Monitor, note four or five errors, three-minute correction slot at the end. Board: the title options on the left, the errors on the right.”

The check before you send it

Could your tutor teach this lesson from your plan, on time, with the materials ready, knowing what to say and what goes on the board? If not, something above is still missing.

Fix this plan

A trainee wrote this for a B1 vocabulary lesson. It breaks at least six of the things you have looked at today. In pairs, find each problem and rewrite the line.

Main aim: To teach vocabulary. Personal aim: To be more confident. Lead-in · 5 min · T–S: The teacher will show some pictures and the students will talk about them. Clarification · 15 min: Teach the words. CCQs. Practice · 10 min · PW / GW / OC: Gap-fill. Anticipated problem (on the lesson plan): Students may not know how to pronounce “cupboard”. Solution: Help them.

Six problems, and one way to fix each

1. The main aim names nothing. Which words, and in what context? “To clarify and practice eight words for kitchen objects (kettle, cupboard, sink…) in the context of moving into a new flat.”

2. The personal aim can’t be checked, and doesn’t come from an action point. “To check my instructions with an ICQ before I hand out the gap-fill (TP2 action point).”

3. The lead-in is prose about the teacher, not instructions to follow. “Show four photos of kitchens. Ss in pairs: which kitchen would you like, and why? 2 min. OCFB: take two answers.”

4. “Teach the words. CCQs.” is a summary. Write out which words, how each one is clarified, and the CCQs themselves.

5. Three interaction patterns for one stage, and no instructions. “Ss do the gap-fill alone (Ind), check in pairs (PW). OCFB.” One pattern per step, honestly recorded.

6. The problem is on the wrong sheet, and “help them” is not a solution. Pronunciation goes on the Language Analysis sheet, with the stress and the sounds marked. A lesson-plan problem would be: “Ss finish the gap-fill at different times. Solution: early finishers write two sentences of their own with the words.”

The checklist to keep

Aims: name the skill or the language exactly; always give the context; vocabulary aims list the items; personal aims come from your last action points. Procedure: bullet points and imperative verbs; write the instruction out; clear who does what, and for how long; a colleague could teach from it. Interaction: one pattern per stage, honestly recorded (Ind · PW · GW · T–S · S–S · OC · OCFB). Anticipated problems: management, tasks, tech and timing on the plan; meaning, form and pronunciation on the LA sheet; every problem gets a real solution.
