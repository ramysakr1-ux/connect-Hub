Evaluating your plan: what changes in the next one

45 minutes

You write a self-evaluation after every teaching practice, and it is assessed every time. Most of them are about how the teacher felt. This one is about whether the document was any good, which is a different question, a different criterion, and the only one that improves the next lesson.

1 · Whose fault was it? 2 · The plan, or the teaching? 3 · Three questions 4 · Rewrite it 5 · Carry it forward 6 · Wrap-up Trainer notes
Whose fault was it? 5 minutes

A lesson goes badly. In pairs, two lists: things the plan did, and things the teacher did. Two minutes, then read a few out.

Most lists come back almost entirely teacher. That imbalance is the session.

The plan, or the teaching? 12 minutes

Six things trainees have actually written after a TP. Which criterion is each one really about: 4n (the plan) or 5m (how you taught)? Two of them are genuinely both.

The three questions 10 minutes

Every plan evaluation answers these, in this order. Anything else is optional; these are not.

Rewrite it 10 minutes

Three sentences from real self-evaluations. Rewrite each so it is about the plan, carries evidence, and says what changes, then compare with a version that does.

Now the real one: open your own last self-evaluation and do the same to it.

Carry it forward 6 minutes

Cambridge’s second bullet for 4n is “address weak areas in the planning of future TP lessons”. So: which part of the next plan does each lesson change?

Wrap-up 2 minutes

Open your next lesson plan now, before you leave. Write one thing into it that came out of your last one: a timing, an instruction in full, an anticipated problem, an extension. One line, in the plan, today.
