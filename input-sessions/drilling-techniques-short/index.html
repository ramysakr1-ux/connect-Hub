Drilling techniques: getting the mouth to say it right

The longer session

Drilling is mechanical, controlled repetition: no message to communicate, just getting a form or sound out correctly and fluently. Do this session before Language Practice: drilling is the first, most controlled step; the next session moves from there toward learners actually using the language.

Drill each other in an unknown language

In pairs: teach your partner one short phrase in a language neither of you has studied (ask the trainer for a phrase, or use one you half-remember). Drill it on them using any two techniques from today. Then swap: they drill you.

The point: experiencing drilling as the actual learner, with zero existing anchor in the language, is the closest trainees will get on this course to feeling what a real beginner feels.

Wrap-up

Drilling produces accurate, fluent form, but it’s not communication yet. That’s exactly where the next session, Language Practice, picks up.
