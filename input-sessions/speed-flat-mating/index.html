Trainer notes
Task 1

The flatmate from hell

On your own: rank these habits from 1 (the worst) to 6 (you could live with it). Then in pairs: compare and explain your top two.

For the trainees watching Watch how I set this up: the instruction, one example, one check, then go. Alone first, then pairs: thinking time comes before talking.
leaves dirty dishes in the sink for days
plays loud music late at night
is always late paying the rent
has friends round every weekend
takes 40-minute showers every morning
eats your food without asking

Discuss: Who do you live with now? Is it easier to live with family, friends or on your own? Why?

Task 2

Words you will hear

Match each word in bold to its meaning.

For the trainees watching This is pre-teaching for the listening, so it is meaning only: I elicit, ask one concept question each, and move on. These six words are the ones that would block the recording. The pair questions then give the words a first use, about the students' own lives.
1. My lodger still hasn't paid for last month.
2. You must wear a label to enter this building.
3. I am always seeking new friends wherever I go.
4. I can buddy up with you if you can't afford the rent alone.
5. Choosy people are hard to please.
6. I'm so braindead from working all day.

a. a name tag or badge  ·  b. looking for  ·  c. picky, hard to please  ·  d. too tired to think  ·  e. a paying guest in your home  ·  f. share a room

In pairs, ask and answer:

  1. Have you ever been a lodger, or had one? How was it?
  2. What are you choosy about: food, clothes, friends?
  3. When do you feel braindead?
  4. Who would you buddy up with on a long trip? Why?
Task 3

What happens at speed flat-mating?

You've heard of speed dating. Now look at the picture. Tick what you think happens at a speed flat-mating evening.

For the trainees watching A prediction task before the first listening. It gives the students a reason to listen and gets their own ideas out first. I don't confirm anything: the recording does that.

Task 7

Get ready for the event

A. What would you ask a future flatmate? Sort the topics: tap a topic to change its color.write each one in a column below.

For the trainees watching Here the lesson turns from listening to speaking. Before the event, the students plan what to ask and how to ask it politely. I clarify the language quickly: meaning, form, pronunciation. Planning time makes the speaking better.
Fine to askAsk with careOff limits
Fine to askAsk with careOff limits

B. Useful language. Use these in your questions and answers.

Asking with care

  • Do you mind me asking what you do?
  • Can I ask whether you smoke?
  • Would you say you're a tidy person?
  • How do you feel about having friends round?

Talking about yourself

  • I tend to cook quite late.
  • I'm the kind of person who washes up straight away.
  • I'm not much of a party person.
  • A deal-breaker for me is smoking indoors.

C. Write five questions you will ask at the event.

Task 8

Who are you tonight?

Your teacher will tell you which card is yours. Fill it in: you can be yourself or invent someone.

For the trainees watching A role gives everyone a reason to talk and something to say, including students who would rather not talk about themselves.

Task 9

The event

Three and a half minutes with each person. When the bell rings, finish your sentence and give them a score. Room-seekers then move one seat.

For the trainees watching This is the main aim, so I step back. I monitor and take notes instead of correcting during the event. We look at good language and errors together at the end.
