Teaching literacy

When the barrier isn't the language. Some learners can't yet read or write fluently in any script, including their own. This session is about noticing that, and adapting; not about ESOL methodology in general, which the rest of the course covers.

plan it
Plan it: the first ten minutes In pairs

A class of six adult beginners. Two of them can’t yet read or write in any script. The aim: to give personal information out loud (name, country, phone number). In pairs, plan the first ten minutes so that nobody depends on reading. Write the steps in order.

One way to plan it

1. Model it yourself: “My name’s Ana. I’m from Brazil.” Point to yourself, then to a photo and a flag. Say it twice.

2. Drill it all together, then one by one, with a gesture for each chunk. Nothing on the board yet.

3. Picture prompts (a face, a flag, a phone) on the wall. Learners say their own lines to a partner.

4. Everyone stands and mingles: “What’s your name? Where are you from?” Answers from memory. Monitor, and help with the sounds.

5. Only now, text: each name written large and clear on the board, in print. Learners copy their own name onto a card; the two who can’t yet write trace over yours.

Why it works: the speaking is secure before any text appears, and reading and writing start from the one word that matters most to each of them, their own name.

Trainer notes Tutor only

Framing this session

Most trainees will never teach a fully pre-literate class on this course. The point isn’t to make them experts; it is to make them notice the possibility before they misread silence as low ability or disengagement.

"Spot the barrier": there is no trick answer

Scenario 3 (can’t hold a pen correctly) is deliberately the one most likely to be misjudged as babyish or irrelevant. It is a real signal in adult pre-literacy classes and worth a beat of discussion.

"Adapt the handout": push past "make it simpler"

The wrong-but-tempting answer is usually the version that is just shorter text; it still assumes fluent decoding. The right answer relies on images and realia doing the work language can’t yet.

Techniques: there is genuine disagreement here

Don’t present the notes as the only right sequencing. Some centers introduce letter names before letter sounds, or vice versa. Use disagreement in the room as the discussion, not a wrong-answer moment.

Time is tight: protect the wrap-up

Time is short for this much ground. If the handout activity runs long, cut to two of the three technique cards rather than skipping the wrap-up question. That question is the takeaway.
