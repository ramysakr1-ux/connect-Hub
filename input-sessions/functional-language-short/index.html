Staging a functional language lesson

A functional lesson doesn’t clarify one grammar structure: it teaches a set of exponents that all achieve the same social function, such as suggesting, complaining, or apologizing. It usually opens with a situation or short dialogue that makes the function’s context obvious. From that context the teacher elicits or presents several exponents at once (“How about...?”, “Why don’t you...?”, “You could...”) rather than a single form. Meaning here is about the social effect (“What is this person trying to get the other person to do?”), and appropriacy matters as much as meaning: which exponent suits a close friend, and which suits a stranger or a boss? Pronunciation work usually centers on intonation, since a technically correct exponent said with the wrong intonation can sound rude or insincere. Practice moves from matching or ordering exponents by formality to freer roleplay in a realistic situation.
