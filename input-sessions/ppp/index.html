Staging a PPP lesson

A PPP lesson opens with the target language given directly, not discovered. The teacher sets up a clear context, models the form once or twice, and checks meaning with CCQs — all before students produce anything themselves. Form comes next, usually a short board summary, then pronunciation: stress, weak forms, drilling chorally and individually. Practice follows in two distinct phases. Controlled practice comes first — gap-fills, substitution drills, matching — with one right answer and the teacher correcting closely as students work. Freer, or production, practice comes last: students use the language to say something of their own, the teacher monitors without interrupting, and feedback on errors is saved for the end. The three stages always run in that order — presentation, then practice, then production — because accuracy work has to come before fluency work in this framework.

Your turn

Stage a page

A page from a made-up coursebook. In pairs, stage it as a PPP lesson for a 45-minute slot with A2 adults: write each stage with its minutes. Then swap with another pair and check theirs against the five questions below.

Unit 5 · Weekend plans · A2

Grammar: be going to. A photo of a family at a station with suitcases. They’re going to visit their grandmother. Look at those clouds! It’s going to rain. The form, shown in a box: subject + am / is / are + going to + base verb.

Exercise 1. Match six plans to six pictures. Exercise 2. Complete eight sentences with going to and a verb in brackets. Exercise 3. In pairs, tell your partner four things you are going to do this weekend, and one you are not going to do.

Check another pair’s plan. Does it have a context in one sentence? Is the meaning checked before the form? Does controlled practice come before freer practice? Does the freer practice get at least ten minutes? Do the minutes add up to 45?

One way to stage the page

Lead-in, 5. Show the station photo. Where are they? Where might they be going? (T–S, then PW.)

Presentation, 10. Context: the family’s plans. Model They’re going to visit their grandmother. CCQs: Have they decided? (Yes.) Is it happening now? (No: soon.) Board the form in chunks. Drill going to as /ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə/ chorally, then individually.

Controlled practice, 12. Exercise 1 in pairs, exercise 2 alone, then check in pairs. One worked item on the board first.

Freer practice, 13. Exercise 3, after one minute alone to plan. Monitor and note errors.

Feedback and correction, 5. Good language first, then three errors on the board for the class to fix.

Where plans usually go wrong

Presentation takes twenty minutes and practice gets what is left. The freer task has no preparation time. The minutes add up to 50. The lesson ends on exercise 2 and the speaking is “if there is time”. In a PPP lesson the practice stages are where the learning happens, so protect their minutes first.
