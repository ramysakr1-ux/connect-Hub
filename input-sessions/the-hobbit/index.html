The Hobbit: good morning

Trainer notes
Task 1

Books and films

Talk with a partner.

For the trainees watching The lead-in brings the topic to life before any text: books against films. I let the students who know the story help the ones who don't.
  1. Do you prefer reading a book or watching the film of it? Why?
  2. Name a book you have read and also seen as a film. Which was better?
  3. What do you know about The Hobbit?
Task 2

The scene

Read our short summary of the scene. Answer the questions.

For the trainees watching Our own summary sets the scene so the passage from the book is easier to follow. It is a short gist task: three questions, one minute.

One quiet morning a hobbit called Bilbo Baggins is standing at the round green door of his home, smoking his pipe and enjoying the sun. A tall old man stops in front of him. He has a long grey beard, a pointed hat and a stick: he is Gandalf, a wizard. Bilbo says good morning, and Gandalf asks him what exactly he means by it. Gandalf says he is looking for someone to join an adventure. Bilbo is not interested at all: adventures, he says, are uncomfortable and make you late for dinner. He tries politely to end the conversation and go back inside, but the old man does not move.

1. Where is Bilbo?
2. Who stops in front of him?
3. Does Bilbo want an adventure?
Task 3

Listen to the book

Your teacher reads the scene from the book. Listen and answer.

For the trainees watching Now the real text, read aloud by me from the book. Listening to literature is hard, so the questions are few and about the main points.
  1. How many times does Bilbo say good morning?
  2. Is the second good morning friendly? Why does he say it?
  3. What does Gandalf want?
Task 4

Watch the film

Your teacher plays the same scene from the film. Don't look at your notes. Was the film…

For the trainees watching The film comes next, with a different task: an opinion, not a right answer. Notice they cannot look at their notes while they watch.

Compare with your group. Do you agree?

Task 5

Same or different?

Watch again. Write two things that are the same in the book and the film, and two that are different.

For the trainees watching A second viewing with a closer task: what is the same and what is different. This is where the detail comes in.
The sameDifferent
Task 6

Good morning, four ways

Listen to your teacher. Then say Good morning in each of these ways. Can your partner guess which one?

For the trainees watching A short focus on intonation: the same two words can be friendly or rude. I model each one; they practise and guess.
1. a friendly helloYou meet a neighbour you like.
2. Go away, pleaseYou want the conversation to end.
3. Really? You again?You are surprised and not happy.
4. It IS a lovely morningThe sun is out and you feel great.
Task 7

Words from the scene

Guess the meaning from the scene first. Then match.

For the trainees watching Vocabulary after the text, not before: they guess from the scene first, then match. Deducing meaning from context is the skill.
1. into the bargain
2. disturbing
3. to take no notice of
4. not quite my sort
5. to gaze
6. to get rid of
7. to move off
8. I suppose
9. that remains to be seen
  1. someone you don’t really like
  2. to look at someone or something for a long time
  3. to go away
  4. I think (but I’m not sure)
  5. also
  6. to ignore
  7. to lose someone or something you don’t want
  8. I don’t know yet
  9. something that interrupts you
Task 8

Your turn

Ask and answer with a partner.

For the trainees watching The new words used about their own lives, so the vocabulary becomes theirs.
  1. What do you find most disturbing when you are working?
  2. Who is not quite your sort, in films or in books?
  3. What would you like to get rid of in your home?
  4. Will you speak perfect English one day? That remains to be seen!
Task 9

A visitor at the door

In pairs. A: you are at home and want a quiet day. B: you knock on the door and invite A on an adventure. A: get rid of B politely. Then change.

For the trainees watching The freer speaking: a role play built on the scene, using the words and the intonation from the lesson. I monitor and note errors for the end.

Use: Good morning… · I suppose… · Not quite my sort… · That remains to be seen… · I really must move off now.
