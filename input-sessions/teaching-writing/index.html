1 · Warm-up: the mystery present

A live activity, no screen needed

Each trainee writes, on a scrap of paper, an interesting present they could give someone in the room — without saying who it's for (online: type it in the chat instead). Collect all the "presents" and redistribute them so nobody gets their own back (online: copy/paste the chat messages and reassign them anonymously, or use breakout room pairs). Ask: what do you do when you receive a present? Elicit: say thank you. Since nobody knows who their present is from, they're going to write a thank-you letter to find out.
2 · The thank-you letter

Show the model letter below. Ask: how does it start? What other features does it have? Is the present actually named — or only implied?

Dear Priya,

Thank you for your fabulous present. When I got it, I couldn't stop laughing. Although I don't smoke, I always have your marvellous gift on the living room table so everyone can use it. It's a lovely present and reminds me of you.

I hope to see you soon.

Best wishes
Sofia.

Task

Trainees write their own thank-you letter for the present they received — implying what it is, never naming it outright. Then everyone mingles, holding their letters up, reading others' letters aloud until they can "deliver" theirs to the person whose present they described (online: post letters in the chat, or read them aloud in breakout room pairs/trios and swap until everyone finds their match).

6 · Wall dictation: Ali and Fatma

Run this as a running dictation (post the text on a wall outside, students run back and forth to relay it) or reveal it here line by line as a reading text (online: share it line by line on screen, or drop it into a breakout room for one student to relay to their group by voice).

Comprehension questions (make full questions from the prompts)

  • Where / Ali and Fatma from?
  • Where / live?
  • Where / work?
  • like television?
  • like football?
  • drink beer?
Answer key
  • Where are Ali and Fatma from? They're from Ankara.
  • Where do they live? They live in Istanbul.
  • Where do they work? They work for Efes, the beer factory.
  • Do they like television? Yes, they do.
  • Do they like football? The text says volleyball, not football; check students catch this.
  • Do they drink beer? No, they don't. They don't like it.
Optional speaking follow-up: turn this into an information gap. One student has the Ali & Fatma text, their partner has only the questions (online: send the text to one student privately via chat, or place them in separate breakout rooms briefly before pairing them up).
7 · Now you write: parallel writing

Task

Using the Ali & Fatma text as a model, write a paragraph about Joe and Barbara from these prompts:
  • Joe and Barbara / from London
  • live / Birmingham
  • work / Cadbury's / chocolate factory
  • Joe and Barbara / like / music / tennis
  • not eat chocolate
  • not like it
Joe and Barbara are from London, but they live in Birmingham. They work for Cadbury's, the chocolate factory. Joe and Barbara like music and tennis. They don't eat chocolate. They don't like it!
