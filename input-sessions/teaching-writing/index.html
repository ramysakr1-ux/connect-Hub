1 · Warm-up: the mystery present

A live activity, no screen needed

On a scrap of paper, write an interesting present you could give someone in the room, without saying who it is for (online: type it in the chat). The presents are collected and handed out again, so nobody gets their own back. What do you do when you receive a present? You say thank you. Nobody knows who their present is from, so you are going to write a thank-you letter to find out.
2 · The thank-you letter

Read the model letter. How does it start? What other features does it have? Is the present actually named, or only implied?

Dear Noor,

Thank you for your splendid present. When I unwrapped it, I couldn't stop smiling. Although I've never had a garden, I hung your lovely gift outside my kitchen window, and now the sparrows visit me every morning. It's a thoughtful present and it reminds me of you.

I hope to see you soon.

Best wishes
Theo.

Task

Write your own thank-you letter for the present you received, implying what it is and never naming it outright. Then everyone mingles, letters held up, reading each other's aloud until you can deliver yours to the person whose present you described (online: post the letters in the chat, or read them aloud in breakout rooms and swap until everyone finds their match).

4 · What do we think about before we write?

A live activity, on paper

Your tutor hands out a short cartoon strip of someone stuck in front of a blank page. Put the panels in order, then talk about the character's attitude to writing: what was the task, and what was it for? The strip is copyrighted, so it is not on this page.

6 · Wall dictation: Tomas and Lena

A running dictation: the text is on a wall outside the room, and one of you runs to it, remembers a line, and comes back to dictate it to your partner, who writes. Swap roles. On screen, the text appears line by line instead.

Comprehension questions (make full questions from the prompts)

  • Where / Tomas and Lena from?
  • Where / live?
  • Where / work?
  • like television?
  • like basketball?
  • eat biscuits?
Answer key
  • Where are Tomas and Lena from? They're from Brno.
  • Where do they live? They live in Prague.
  • Where do they work? They work for Sunbright, the biscuit factory.
  • Do they like television? Yes, they do.
  • Do they like basketball? The text says handball, not basketball; check students catch this.
  • Do they eat biscuits? No, they don't. They don't like them.
Optional speaking follow-up: turn this into an information gap. One student has the Tomas & Lena text, their partner has only the questions (online: send the text to one student privately via chat, or place them in separate breakout rooms briefly before pairing them up).
7 · Now you write: parallel writing

Task

Using the Tomas & Lena text as a model, write a paragraph about Dan and Ruth from these prompts:
  • Dan and Ruth / from Leeds
  • live / Bristol
  • work / Marlowe's / toy factory
  • Dan and Ruth / like / cinema / cycling
  • not buy toys
  • not like it
Dan and Ruth are from Leeds, but they live in Bristol. They work for Marlowe's, the toy factory. Dan and Ruth like cinema and cycling. They don't buy toys. They don't like them!

Write the model first Your turn

Before you set a writing task, write it yourself, at the level your learners will. In pairs, write the answer an A2 learner could give to this task. Then underline the four or five chunks you would have to teach first.

The task · A2

Write a message to a friend to invite them to your birthday. Say where and when it is, and what to bring. Ask for an answer. About 40 to 60 words.

One model, and the chunks in it

Hi Lena, I’m having a birthday dinner at my flat on Saturday 12th at 7 pm. It’s 14 Park Road. Can you come? Please bring a dessert if you can. Let me know by Thursday. See you soon! Kasia

Chunks to teach first: I’m having…, Can you come?, Please bring…, Let me know by…, See you soon!

Why write it first

You find out how long the task really takes, and whether 40 to 60 words is possible at this level. You see which language the task needs before your learners do. And you have a model to show them, and a way to check your own instructions: if you could not do the task from the instructions, they could not.
