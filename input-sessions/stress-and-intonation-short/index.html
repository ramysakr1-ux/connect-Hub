Where this feeds back in

FOL asks you to record and analyze a learner's pronunciation errors: stress and intonation are two of the categories you'll be sorting those errors into. LRT asks you to analyze language for teaching, including its phonological features: the word-stress marking convention practiced here is the one your LRT write-up should use.

Intonation: question or statement? 8 minutes

The words don't change, only the tune does. "You're coming." falling means a statement; rising means a question: click each line to hear the tune, then guess before revealing.

Marking stress for LRT 12 minutes

The convention: a bold syllable, or a small mark before it: record vs record. Click a word to hear it, then click the syllable you think carries the stress.

Primary and secondary stress 10 minutes

Longer words often carry two stresses, not one: a strong primary stress and a weaker secondary stress. Click Play to hear the word said naturally, then listen for the two stresses yourself, or say the word aloud. Click each circle once for secondary stress, twice for primary.

Extension, if there's time: stress carries attitude

Same sentence, same words: the stressed word changes what's actually meant. Read the context, then click the word you'd stress in the reply. Reveal it to read why, and hear the reply said that way.
