And the context

The overall context is the situation you build: what the students see, and the question you ask to pull the sentence out of them. "Travel" is a topic, not a context.

Overall context of the lessonA world map with three pins in it: Italy, Egypt, Peru. These are places in my life. Japan has no pin: not yet. "Italy, in my life, yes. When? It's not important. So how do I say it? I ___ to Italy."
Name of the structurePresent perfect simple for life experience, at an unspecified time. Affirmative, negative, and questions with ever.
Marker sentence(s)I've been to Italy. I haven't been to Japan. Have you ever been to Egypt?
The testCould a colleague pick up your page and run the first two minutes of your lesson from it? If not, it isn't specific enough yet.
Meaning analysis, then clarification

Two boxes that ask two different things. People answer the second one twice.

Meaning analysisWhat the language means, here, in words a student could follow. Not the grammar label, not the form.
Clarification techniqueHow you will clarify the meaning in the lesson and check they have understood: your CCQs with their answers, and any timeline, cline or picture.

Are these good concept checking questions?

Same sentence: I've been to Italy.

What makes a CCQ workShort. Yes or no, either/or, or a one-word answer. Simpler language than the thing you are checking, and never the target language itself. In Lite, every question has its answer beside it in brackets.
Form analysis

Lite's hint: parts of speech, word order, rules, positive, negative and question forms. That means what goes on the board, not the name of the tense.

A form box needs more than one line

Before you look: what else belongs in this form box besides the line you just built? Write it, then compare.

Form analysis + subject + have/has + past participle: I have been to Italy. Contracted: I've been, she's been, the form they will hear.
− subject + haven't/hasn't + past participle: I haven't been to Japan.
? have/has + subject + ever + past participle: Have you ever been to Egypt? Short answers: Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
been, not gone: been means went and came back. Irregular, so it is learned, not worked out.
Form problems and solutionsProblem: I have been to Italy in 2019.
Solution: Pre-empt it in a CCQ ("Do we say the exact time? (No)"), then board the wrong sentence at the error correction stage and let them repair it.
Transcription and pronunciation features

Two boxes. The first is the marker sentence in phonemic script with the stress marked; in Lite you click the symbols from the bar at the bottom of the page. The second names what happens to the sounds, and the problems box under it says how you will show it.

Phonetic transcription and stress/aɪv ˈbiːn tʊ ˈɪtəli/

Which feature is each note about?

Phonetic transcription and stress/aɪv ˈbiːn tə ˈɪtəli/
Pronunciation featuresContraction: I have becomes I've /aɪv/. Weak form: to is /tə/, not /tuː/. Sentence stress on been and Italy. In the question, have is weak, /həv/, and the voice rises at the end.
Pronunciation problems and solutionsProblem: Ss say the full /hæv/ and stress it.
Solution: Model the contraction only, back-chain from Italy, and show I have becoming I've with finger highlighting.
The giveawayIf your pronunciation box could go on any other lesson's page without changing a word, you haven't analyzed this language. You've described drilling.
Problems: which box?

Lite gives you a problems-and-solutions box after meaning, after form and after pronunciation. Problems with tasks, materials, timing, technology and classroom management go on the lesson plan itself, not here.

Ss may confuse been and gone.I will explain the difference.Contrast two board sentences, He's been to the shop (he's here) and He's gone to the shop (he's not here), and check with "Where is he now?"

Source

Every grammar and functional block has a Source box. It is not decoration. Which of these would you accept?

Four worth owning or bookmarkingSwan, Practical English Usage, for "is this right, and why?" Parrott, Grammar for English Language Teachers, written for teachers, with the problems learners have. Scrivener, Teaching English Grammar, with teaching ideas attached. A learner's dictionary online, Cambridge or Oxford, for transcription, stress and definitions at the right level.

The last check

One habit worth havingWrite the language analysis before you write the procedure. If you can't say clearly what the language means, how it's formed and how it sounds, you can't plan the stages that clarify it, and it shows in the lesson, not just on the page.
