Answer key: exponent 1 Reveal
“Can you pass me the sugar, please?”
Form
can + subject pronoun + infinitive verb (no ‘to’) + object pronoun + definite article + noun — present simple
Function
A polite request
Phonology
/kən/ weak form; /kənju/ linking of ‘can’ + ‘you’
CCQs
Is it past, present or future? (Present) · Does she have the sugar? (No) · Does she want the sugar? (Yes)
Context
Two friends are drinking tea in a café. The table is a bit long and the other friend can't reach the sugar, so they need to ask for it.

Answer key: exponent 2 Reveal
“You should stay in bed.”
Form
subject pronoun + should + infinitive verb (no ‘to’) + prepositional phrase (in + noun) — modal verb, present
Function
Giving advice
Phonology
‘should’ weak form /ʃəd/; sentence stress on ‘stay’ and ‘bed’; falling intonation
CCQs
Is the person ill? (Yes) · Is this an order or a suggestion? (Suggestion) · Does the speaker think it’s a good idea? (Yes) · Must they stay in bed? (No)
Context
A friend has a bad cold but is thinking of going to work anyway. Their flatmate tells them it’s better to rest.
answer key
Answer key: exponent 3 Reveal
“If only I'd been more romantic with her.”
Form
If only + subject pronoun + had (‘d) + past participle + comparative adjective + prepositional phrase — past perfect
Function
Expressing regret about the past
Phonology
‘I’d’ contracted /aɪd/; ‘been’ weak form /bɪn/; stress on ‘only’ and ‘romantic’; falling intonation
CCQs
Is he talking about the past? (Yes) · Was he romantic with her? (No) · Can he change it now? (No) · Does he feel sorry about it? (Yes)
Context
A man is telling a friend about his ex-girlfriend, who has just left him. He’s thinking about what he could have done differently.
Trainer notes Tutor only

Before

The three exponents on the board before they arrive: "Can you pass me the sugar, please?", "You should stay in bed.", "If only I’d been more romantic with her." The Wordwall link in part 6 is a phone version of the matching; use it if the room has phones and the page is on the projector.

Running it

StageWhat you do
Exponent 1, togetherAnalyse the sugar sentence as a class: form as a chunk (not a grammar label), function, phonology, CCQs, context. Reveal the key and compare line by line. This is the model for the other two.
Exponents 2 and 3Pairs: one takes "You should stay in bed", the other "If only I’d been more romantic", then they teach each other. Keys are at the end of the page (parts 10 and 11), not beside the task, on purpose.
The functionsTwelve functions; for each, one exponent they know and one other way of saying it. Thirty seconds a function, out loud, no writing.
MatchingIndividually on the page or on Wordwall, then the key (part 7).
RequestsSix exponents of the same request. Rank them from most to least formal on the board, then ask who would say each to whom. Appropriacy is the point: a request can be perfect grammar and still wrong.
RespondingThe gap is "respond". Collect accepting and declining exponents from the room before revealing the examples.

The key

On the page: parts 2, 7, 10 and 11, and the examples in part 9. The gap in part 9 accepts respond, reply or answer.

Watch for

  • Form written as a grammar lesson ("modal verb can + subject…"). For functional language the form is the chunk: "Can you + verb + me the + noun".
  • CCQs about grammar instead of function. "Is she asking politely?" is a function CCQ; "Is it present simple?" is not.
  • "If only I’d been more romantic": trainees analyse the third conditional and forget the regret. The function is the headline.
