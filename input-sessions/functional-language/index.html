Answer key: exponent 1 Reveal
“Can you pass me the salt, please?”
Form
can + subject pronoun + infinitive verb (no ‘to’) + object pronoun + definite article + noun — present simple
Function
A polite request
Phonology
/kən/ weak form; /kənju/ linking of ‘can’ + ‘you’
CCQs
Is it past, present or future? (Present) · Does she have the salt? (No) · Does she want the salt? (Yes)
Context
Two friends are eating lunch in a café. The table is quite wide and one friend can't reach the salt, so they need to ask for it.

Requests: what else do learners need to know? Discussion

Imagine you're teaching all of these exponents for requests in the same lesson. What else do learners need to understand about them — formality, appropriacy, the relationship between speakers? How would you clarify this in class?

  • Can you pass me the salt?
  • Would you be so kind as to pass me the salt, please?
  • Pass the salt, please.
  • Salt.
  • Could you pass me the salt?
  • Would you mind passing the salt?

Points to cover

Formality. From “Salt.” (family only, and abrupt even there) through “Pass the salt, please.” and “Can you pass me the salt?” (everyday, neutral) to “Could you…?”, “Would you mind passing…?” and “Would you be so kind as to…?” (more polite and more distant; among friends the last one can sound sarcastic).

Appropriacy. Who is asking whom, and how big the favor is, decide the choice. Passing the salt is a small favor, so the most formal ones sound odd at a family table.

Tone. Intonation and “please” carry politeness as much as the words do. A flat “Could you pass the salt” can still sound rude.

Form and the answer. “Would you mind” takes -ing, and you agree by saying no: “No, not at all.”

In class. Put the six on a cline from least to most polite; learners place them. Then give situation cards (your brother, your manager, a stranger on a train) and they choose which to use, and say it.

Answer key: exponent 2 Reveal
“You should rest at home.”
Form
subject pronoun + should + infinitive verb (no ‘to’) + prepositional phrase (at + noun) — modal verb, present
Function
Giving advice
Phonology
‘should’ weak form /ʃəd/; sentence stress on ‘rest’ and ‘home’; falling intonation
CCQs
Is the person ill? (Yes) · Is this an order or a suggestion? (Suggestion) · Does the speaker think it’s a good idea? (Yes) · Must they rest at home? (No)
Context
A friend has a bad cold but is thinking of going to work anyway. Their flatmate tells them it’s better to rest.
answer key
Answer key: exponent 3 Reveal
“If only I'd been more patient with him.”
Form
If only + subject pronoun + had (‘d) + past participle + comparative adjective + prepositional phrase — past perfect
Function
Expressing regret about the past
Phonology
‘I’d’ contracted /aɪd/; ‘been’ weak form /bɪn/; stress on ‘only’ and ‘patient’; falling intonation
CCQs
Is she talking about the past? (Yes) · Was she patient with him? (No) · Can she change it now? (No) · Does she feel sorry about it? (Yes)
Context
A woman is telling a friend about her brother, who has just moved away after an argument. She’s thinking about what she could have done differently.
