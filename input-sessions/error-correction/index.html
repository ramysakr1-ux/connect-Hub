      Trainer notes Tutor only

      Before

      Check the sound for the lead-in film (the Mitchell and Webb sketch, 3 minutes). The two practice tables print from the handout; a printed copy per pair saves the typing.

      Running it

      MinStageWhat you do
      3Lead-inRead the two viewing questions aloud before you press play. After the film, pairs compare; take one or two answers to each. The errors are small and the correcting is relentless: that contrast is the session.
      7AttitudesEach trainee ranks the nine alone, then compares in threes. No key. Draw out the two that split a room: "You’ll never learn if you are not corrected" and "Making a mistake means you’ve learnt it wrongly".
      8The decision flowPart A in pairs on screen; the order is type, deal or not, when, who, technique. Part B the same. Ask which decision they skip in TP: usually "who": the teacher always does it.
      7Match the errorsIndividually, then check. The eight are a mix on purpose: three grammar, three pronunciation, two vocabulary. Ask which ones a student could self-correct if pointed at, and which need telling.
      8How to correctClick the twelve as a class. Demonstrate three on a volunteer: finger correction, the echo with a rising tone, the one-word question. They need to see them, not read them.
      12Practice 1 and 2Pairs, one table each, then swap and check each other’s. The key is below; the page has none for these two tables, so it is yours to give.

      The key: the practice tables

      CorrectedTypeA technique
      AThey can swim very well.Grammar: modal + bare infinitiveFinger correction, take out "to"
      BShe went to the cinema yesterday.Grammar: word orderRepeat up to the error, rising tone
      CHe’s reading a book.Grammar: articleFinger correction, a missing finger
      DHow many photographs did you take?Grammar: countable, much or manyOne-word question: "Countable?"
      EShe comes to school late every day.Grammar: third person -sGesture: the S drawn in the air
      FI waited two hours for the bus yesterday.Grammar: past simpleTimeline, or "Yesterday?" with a hand behind the shoulder
      GCan I borrow your pen?Appropriacy: too formal between friends"Your friend? Really?" and a raised eyebrow
      H…because she was pregnant.Vocabulary: false friend (embarazada)Tell them, with the smile; then CCQ the two words
      II’m bored.Vocabulary: -ed and -ing adjectivesEcho "I’m boring?" and point at yourself
      J…by ship.Pronunciation: /iː/ and /ɪ/Phonemic chart, or draw the sheep
      K…a boyfriend in England.Pronunciation: the diphthongModel and drill the one word
      LI really enjoy your lessons.Grammar: enjoy is a verb, not an adjectiveBoard the sentence, let them find it
      MHe’s watching TV.Pronunciation: /tʃ/ and /ʃ/Minimal pair on the board: wash, watch

      Watch for

      • Trainees who correct everything in their TPs the week after this session. The attitudes task is where to plant "not every error, not every time".
      • The decision flow learnt as a list. Ask for it as a story: "A student says X. What do you decide first?"

      Sources. The attitude statements, the error examples ("Give me one butterbread!", "I'm going to heat you") and the practice tables follow Mark Bartram and Richard Walton, Correction: Mistake Management (Language Teaching Publications, 1991). The decision flow and the correction ideas draw on Jim Scrivener, Learning Teaching (Macmillan), chapter on feedback and correction. Lead-in video: That Mitchell and Webb Look, series 4 (BBC).
