      Trainer notes Tutor only

      Before

      Check the sound for the lead-in film (the Mitchell and Webb sketch, 3 minutes). The two practice tables print from the handout; a printed copy per pair saves the typing.

      Running it

      StageWhat you do
      Lead-inRead the two viewing questions aloud before you press play. After the film, pairs compare; take one or two answers to each. The errors are small and the correcting is relentless: that contrast is the session.
      AttitudesEach trainee ranks the nine alone, then compares in threes. No key. Draw out the two that split a room: "You will not improve unless somebody corrects you" and "A slip means you’ve learnt it the wrong way".
      The decision flowPart A in pairs on screen; the order is type, deal or not, when, who, technique. Part B the same. Ask which decision they skip in TP: usually "who": the teacher always does it.
      Match the errorsIndividually, then check. The eight are a mix on purpose: three grammar, three pronunciation, two vocabulary. Ask which ones a student could self-correct if pointed at, and which need telling.
      How to correctClick the twelve as a class. Demonstrate three on a volunteer: finger correction, the echo with a rising tone, the one-word question. They need to see them, not read them.
      Practice 1 and 2Pairs, one table each, then swap and check each other’s. The key is below; the page has none for these two tables, so it is yours to give.

      The key: the practice tables

      CorrectedTypeA technique
      AWe must leave early today.Grammar: modal + bare infinitiveFinger correction, take out "to"
      BHe visited the museum yesterday.Grammar: word orderRepeat up to the error, rising tone
      CShe’s wearing a hat.Grammar: articleFinger correction, a missing finger
      DHow many bottles did you buy?Grammar: countable, much or manyOne-word question: "Countable?"
      EHe walks to work slowly every day.Grammar: third person -sGesture: the S drawn in the air
      FI stayed three days in the hotel last week.Grammar: past simpleTimeline, or "Last week?" with a hand behind the shoulder
      GCan I borrow your umbrella?Appropriacy: too formal between friends"Your friend? Really?" and a raised eyebrow
      H…because I had a cold.Vocabulary: false friend (constipado)Tell them, with the smile; then CCQ the two words
      II’m bored.Vocabulary: -ed and -ing adjectivesEcho "I’m boring?" and point at yourself
      J…fill the tank on Fridays.Pronunciation: /iː/ and /ɪ/Phonemic chart, or draw a full tank
      K…to bake cakes on Sundays.Pronunciation: the diphthongModel and drill the one word
      LAt the party she enjoyed herself a lot.Grammar: enjoy is a verb, not an adjectiveBoard the sentence, let them find it
      MPlease bring a chair.Pronunciation: /tʃ/ and /ʃ/Minimal pair on the board: share, chair

      Watch for

      • Trainees who correct everything in their TPs the week after this session. The attitudes task is where to plant "not every error, not every time".
      • The decision flow learnt as a list. Ask for it as a story: "A student says X. What do you decide first?"

      Sources. The attitude statements, the errors and the practice tables are made up for this session. The ideas of a decision flow and of correction techniques are standard in teacher training; see Mark Bartram and Richard Walton, Correction: Mistake Management (Language Teaching Publications, 1991), and Jim Scrivener, Learning Teaching (Macmillan). Lead-in video: That Mitchell and Webb Look, series 4 (BBC).
