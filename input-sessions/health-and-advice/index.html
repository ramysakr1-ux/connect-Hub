Health: kind words and advice

Trainer notes
Task 1

How are you feeling?

In pairs: how many health problems do you know in English? Write them. Which are serious, and which are not?

For the trainees watching The lead-in activates what they already know. A brainstorm in pairs gets everyone talking before I ask for anything.
Not seriousSerious
Task 2

What's the problem?

Read the six conversations. Choose the problem for each one.

For the trainees watching The six conversations would be on the walls: a gallery reading gets them out of their seats. The first task is gist only: which problem?

a hangover  ·  a sprained ankle  ·  food poisoning  ·  toothache  ·  sunburn  ·  a sore throat

Task 3

Six conversations

Choose the problem. Then complete the advice with a word from the box.

For the trainees watching A test stage: can they already give advice? What they get wrong tells me what to teach in the next steps.

rest  ·  drink  ·  put  ·  see  ·  taking  ·  call

Omar: Why are you walking like that?

Lena: I twisted my foot playing football yesterday and now it's really swollen.

Omar: Oh, you poor thing. You should it on a cushion and put some ice on it.

B

Kai: You look terrible this morning.

Mia: It was Sam's birthday. I don't remember getting home, and my head is killing me.

Kai: Well, it serves you right! Why don't you lots of water?

C

Tom: I fell asleep on the beach for three hours. It really hurts.

Task 4

Kind or not?

Mark each sentence S (sympathetic) or U (unsympathetic). Then say them with a partner: sound as kind, or as unkind, as you can.

For the trainees watching Functional language lives in the voice. Sympathetic or not is mostly intonation, so I drill it after they decide.
1. Oh well, if you hadn't drunk so much, you wouldn't be feeling so bad now, would you?
2. Oh dear, you poor thing.
3. Oh yes, I know what you mean.
4. Oh well, it serves you right then, doesn't it?
5. Oh well, you've only got yourself to blame, haven't you?
6. That must be awful.
Task 5

The language

Read the box. Add one more phrase to each column.

For the trainees watching The language is collected in one box after they have met it, not before.

Kind

  • Oh dear, you poor thing.
  • That must be awful.
  • I know what you mean.

Not so kind

  • It serves you right!
  • You've only got yourself to blame.
  • If you hadn't…, you wouldn't…

Advice

  • You should / ought to + verb
  • Why don't you + verb?
  • If I were you, I'd + verb
  • Have you tried + -ing?
  • You'd better + verb
Task 6

Tell me your problem

Stand up. Your teacher gives you a card. Tell classmates your problem. They react, kindly or not, and give advice. Then change cards.

For the trainees watching A controlled mingle: lots of repetition of the same phrases with different partners. I correct on the spot here, because accuracy is the aim of this stage.
  1. You ate a whole cake last night. Now you feel sick.
  2. You have a terrible cold and a big exam tomorrow.
  3. You stayed out until 4 a.m. and you have work at 9.
  4. You cut your finger cooking and it won't stop bleeding.
  5. You can't sleep: your neighbour's dog barks all night.
  6. You've had a headache for three days.
Task 7

You're the expert

In groups, discuss.

For the trainees watching Now fluency: a discussion with no fixed language. I don't interrupt; I note errors for later.
  1. What is the best thing to do for a cold?
  2. Do you go to the doctor quickly, or wait?
  3. Do you believe in home remedies? Which ones?
  4. Is it better to be sympathetic, or honest, when a friend is ill because of something they did?
Task 8

Dear Liz

In pairs: one of you runs to the letter on the wall, remembers a line, comes back and says it. Your partner writes. Change after each line.

For the trainees watching A running dictation: reading, speaking, listening and writing in one activity, and it wakes the room up.
Task 9

Write back

In pairs, write Liz's reply: be kind, and give three pieces of advice.

For the trainees watching The lesson ends in writing, using the sympathy and advice language from the whole lesson. I collect errors for the delayed correction.
