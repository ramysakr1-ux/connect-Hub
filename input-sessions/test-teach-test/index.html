Before anyone explains anything: where did you hesitate? Tell your partner which pairs you were unsure of, and why.

Test, teach, test

1. Who already knew what. Some pairs went in without a thought; others made everyone stop. The teacher found out which of the five uses the class could already handle and which they could not, before teaching any of them.

2. Taught to the gaps. The uses the class had got right were named and moved past; the ones that caused the hesitation got the time. The teaching came out of your mistakes, not out of a plan written before the teacher had met you.

3. To measure the difference. The same kind of task, after the teaching, shows the learners and the teacher what has changed. A different kind of task would show something else.

4. Test, teach, test. Harmer calls the first stage the deep end: the learners are put in the water before they have been taught to swim, and then taught what the swimming showed they needed.

Test-teach-test

Stage 1. The teacher introduces the target language and the students’ current knowledge of it. The teacher could do this in ways: getting the students to select the correct word or structure to complete a sentence from a choice, filling gaps with a suitable word or structure, discussing the differences in meaning between similar sentences with different structures, or finding matching responses for sentences from a choice of several.

Stage 2. The teacher then uses the from the first stage to teach or revise the target language. The teacher could do this by getting the students to arrange words or structures on a cline, draw their own , or discuss the answers to concept checking questions on a handout.

Stage 3. The teacher then gets the students to do controlled practice exercises. Having from their correction and had their awareness raised in the teach stage, the students should do the exercises better. They will often do activities like the ones in stage 1. After this controlled stage the teacher will usually go on to freer speaking.

Which stage? Sort them

Fourteen things a teacher might do. Which stage of a test-teach-test lesson could each one be, and which three have no place in one at all? A test task can be the first test or the controlled second test, so those two share an answer.

The three that do not belongReading a text for the gist is how a text-based lesson starts, not a test of one structure. A teacher repeating the rules from the front is a lecture, not a teach stage built on what the test showed. Five random sentences test nothing and say nothing: a second test has to let the learners use the language for something.
Which approach? Guided discovery or test-teach-test

Ten things teachers have said about these two ways of teaching grammar. Which approach is each one about? Decide with a partner, then check.

What they shareBoth put the learners to work before the teacher explains. Guided discovery gives them a text and questions that lead to the rule; test-teach-test gives them a task and teaches from how it went. Neither is for skills lessons: both are ways of teaching grammar or vocabulary.

One way to do it

Test. Pairs. Each student has a picture of the same hotel room, and must not show it. The pictures are the same except that five everyday objects are in picture A and not in B, and five others in B and not in A. They describe where their objects are, and draw what the partner describes. You walk round and note what they say: which prepositions they reach for, which they avoid, which they get wrong, and who says in the wall for a picture.

Teach. Pairs compare pictures and remember how they described each place. Some sentences go on the board, theirs and yours from your notes. The class decides which are right and which are not, and why. You steer, and only settle what the class cannot settle. A quick drawing or the room itself clears up opposite and between. Nothing is taught that the test showed they already had.

Test. The same task with a different room, and then something freer: describe your own kitchen to your partner, who draws it. You listen for the five objects that went wrong the first time.

Scrivener’s own example lesson, in Learning Teaching. His warning with it: a first test that is a free speaking task will throw up problems you did not plan for, which is why teachers tend to try test-teach-test once they know the grammar well enough to teach what comes up.
