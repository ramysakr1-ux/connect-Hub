Teaching mixed-ability classes

Every class is a mixed-level class; the question is how close you are standing. Why classes are mixed, what one text can do at three levels, the ten things a teacher can vary, early finishers, keeping everyone in, what is realistic, and then one stage of your own TP, differentiated three ways.

Your TP group Lead-in

Think of the group you teach now. On each line, where is your weakest student, and where is your strongest? Drag the two ends. Then compare with someone who teaches the same group.

What is the widest gap? Is it the same student at the bottom of every line?

How close are you standing?From across the room a class is “pre-intermediate”. Stand nearer and it is weak, middling and strong pre-intermediate, with a couple from the level above and below. Stand next to one person and she has good grammar, thinner vocabulary and much weaker listening. Every learner has a range of levels, so every class is a mixed-level class. Planning gives you one lesson for all of them; teaching is noticing and adjusting, person by person, as it runs.

One text, three levels Match the lesson to the level

A short story a teacher recorded, and three teachers who used the same recording with three different classes. Which lesson is for which level, and what tells you?

The most important day of my life was the day I met Hana. A friend was having a party, but it was raining so hard that I nearly stayed at home. My friend introduced us and we talked all evening. Hana was only in the city for a few days; she lives in Osaka, and she was flying home the next morning. But that morning the airport closed because of a storm. She called me, we had lunch, and by the time she flew home two days later we knew. We got married eighteen months after that.

What changed, and what did notThe recording was the same. What changed was the support before listening, what the learners had to do with it, how many times they heard it, and what they did afterward. That is the pattern for everything in this session: keep the material, vary the task.
What you can vary Sort them

Ten things teachers do in mixed classes. What is being varied in each?

The cheap ones and the dear onesDifferent content costs you a second set of materials and a harder feedback stage. Different tasks on the same content cost a line on the plan. Different support and different responses cost nothing but attention. Harmer’s advice is to spend most of your effort on the cheap ones.
Early finishers Challenge or busywork?

Six students have finished the gap-fill with five minutes to go. Four things you could give them. Which are a real challenge, and which are just more of the same?

The early finisher“The early finisher is on the way to becoming a discipline problem; the slow learner never finishes anything and gets demoralised.” Plan the extension before the lesson, make it feel like a reward, and let the slower students finish the main task properly: both halves of the class need something.
Everyone in Group work

Groups of four are preparing a short presentation. In every group the two strongest students do the work and the other two watch. Six things you could do. Which keep everyone in, and which leave someone out?

Who gets your timeSpend it all with the weaker students and the stronger ones switch off; spend it all with the stronger ones and the weaker feel ignored. Start every task at a level everyone can join, then differentiate. Students who feel excluded soon behave as if they are.

A flexible version

A present perfect gap-fill, for example

As it is. Eight sentences, a verb in brackets at the end of each, present perfect for experience: I ___ (never / eat) sushi.

Stronger. No brackets: the verbs are gone and they choose their own. Then two sentences of their own that are true.

Weaker. The eight verbs in a box at the top, the first one done, and the participles given for the two irregular ones.

Early finishers. Turn four of the sentences into questions and ask your partner. A reason to stand up, and real use.

Flexible. “In three minutes, write as many true sentences as you can with ever or never. Twelve is the record.” Everyone writes; nobody is finished.

The material did not change. The plan gained three lines. The feedback stage is the same for everyone, because the sentences are.
