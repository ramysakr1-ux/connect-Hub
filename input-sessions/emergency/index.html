It’s an emergency!

Trainer notes
Task 1

What would you do?

Answer the questions with a partner. Give reasons.

For the trainees watching The lead-in is personal: how would you react? It gets the topic and the feelings vocabulary going before any text.
Task 2

How do you eat an elephant?

Guess the answer with your partner. What do you think it means in an emergency?

For the trainees watching A short prediction. The title is a riddle, so the students want to read to find the answer.
Task 3

Read the article

Read the article on p. 45. Answer the questions.

For the trainees watching Reading for the main ideas: three questions only. Pairs compare before I take answers.
  1. What is the key to surviving a life-or-death crisis?
  2. What is the 10-80-10 principle?
  3. Apart from keeping calm, what else is important?
Task 4

Words from the article

Find the highlighted words in the article. Match them to the meanings.

For the trainees watching Vocabulary after the reading: they find the words in context first, then match. Meaning first, then pronunciation where it is hard.
1. unable to react because the situation is too strong
2. so shocked that you can’t think clearly or act
3. a new or difficult thing that tests your abilities
4. very confused
5. based on reason, not emotion
6. to succeed in dealing with a problem
7. to not get excited or nervous; not to panic
8. pieces of work you are able to deal with
Task 5

Strong feelings

Listen to the dialogues. Choose the stressed syllable in each adjective. Then practise the dialogues with a partner.

For the trainees watching Strong adjectives live in the stress. I drill them with the dialogues, so the voice carries the feeling.

Task 6

It's an emergency!

In threes: you are A, B or C. Read only your text. Note what you should and shouldn't do, and why.

For the trainees watching A jigsaw reading: each student has a different text, so they have a real reason to tell the others.

Student A · an emergency on a plane

You should…You shouldn’t…

Student B · lost on a hike in the mountains

You should…You shouldn’t…

Student C · somebody breaks into your house

You should…You shouldn’t…
Task 7

Tell your group

In your own words, tell the others how to survive your emergency. Don't read. They listen and ask one question each.

For the trainees watching Retelling in their own words, not reading aloud. This is where the reading becomes speaking.

Use: The most important thing is… · You should never… · Experts say… · If you…, you'll…

Task 8

The rules of survival

In groups, rank the rules from 1 (most important) to 8. You must all agree.

For the trainees watching A ranking task forces them to agree, which means they have to explain and persuade.
Stay calm and breathe.
Have a plan before anything happens.
Stay where you are and wait for help.
Tell someone where you are going.
Do one thing at a time.
Always carry water and a phone.
Trust your instincts.
Never try to be a hero.
Task 9

What now?

In groups: choose an emergency. Plan exactly what you would do, step by step. Then tell the class. The class decides: would you survive?

For the trainees watching The freest stage: their own plan for a new emergency, told to the class. I note errors for the end.
  1. You are stuck in a lift with five strangers. The alarm doesn’t work.
  2. Your kitchen is on fire and the smoke is getting thick.
  3. You lose your little cousin at a big music festival.
  4. Your car breaks down on a desert road at night, with no phone signal.
