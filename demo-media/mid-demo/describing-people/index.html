The language comes from the reading

This is the second demonstration lesson your tutors teach the A2 class on day one, straight after Sarah's date. Nothing new is brought in to start it: the class goes back to the sentence Sarah used to describe her date and works out what the words mean before anyone explains them.

Watch how the meaning is checked before the form, and how much of the lesson the students do in pairs.

Level
A2
Main aim
To clarify and practice language for describing appearance (has + adjective + noun, is + adjective, wears) in the context of Sarah's date
Subsidiary aim
To give students fluency practice in describing people they know
Watch forThe language lesson starts from a sentence the students have already read and understood. Watch how the tutor links back to the reading, so the meaning is there before the form.
Step 1 · Transition

Let's look at the language Sarah used

From the reading lesson, Sarah talking to Priya:

"Well, he's tall with brown hair and green eyes. And he has a beard."

Watch forNobody explains the words yet. Watch how the students work out the meaning from context in pairs, and how the tutor guides with questions instead of giving answers.
Step 2 · Guided discovery

What do the underlined words mean?

In pairs, look at the text again and guess the meaning from context. Don't check a dictionary yet.

He's tall with brown hair and green eyes. And he has a beard.

hair color?eye color?face: what is it?
Answersbrown hair: the color of his hair. green eyes: the color of his eyes. beard: the hair on his chin and cheeks.
Watch forThe pictures check meaning without translation or a long explanation. Watch how the instructions are given and checked before the students start.

Step 5 · Clarify form

Find the pattern

Look at the sentences, then complete each pattern.

He's tall with brown hair and green eyes. He has a beard. He's bald. She has curly hair.

On the board
PatternExamples
has + adjective + nounhas curly hair, has brown eyes
has + a + nounhas a beard, has a mustache

with = and hasHe's tall with brown hair.
Watch forThe students listen and decide before anything is marked. Then watch the drilling: the model said naturally, the whole class repeating, then individuals, and the stress marked on the board.

Step 7 · Clarify

Two questions that sound the same

They look similar, but they ask for completely different things. Read the two questions, then decide where each answer belongs.

What does he look like?

His appearance: what you can see.

He's tall with brown hair.

What is he like?

His personality: what kind of person he is.

He's friendly and very funny.

Which question does each answer go with?

Watch forThe students choose first and the rule comes after. Watch whether the rule comes from them.
Step 8 · Clarify

Which order?

We put these adjectives in a fixed order in English. Choose the one that sounds right.

The orderLength or size, then style, then color, then the noun: long straight black hair, big green eyes.
Watch forThe students add words of their own. Watch how the tutor collects them, and which ones go on the board.

Step 10 · Controlled practice

has, is or wears?

Choose the right verb for each sentence.

Rememberhas / has got + hair, eyes, a beard. is / are + tall, short, bald, slim. wears + glasses.
Watch forStill controlled, but now written, about real pictures. Watch how the tutor monitors the writing and helps individuals.
Step 11 · Controlled practice

What do they look like?

Write one sentence for each person. Example: Brad has long blond hair.

Possible answers
  1. Brad has long blond hair.
  2. Jessica has red hair and wears glasses.
  3. Matthew is bald. He has a beard and wears glasses.
  4. Ravi has short gray hair and a mustache.
  5. Angela has short gray hair.
  6. Amy is short with curly dark hair. Paul is tall with short dark hair.
Watch forFreer practice: using the language for something real. Watch whether the tutor steps back, and how the feedback afterward deals with content first and language second.
Step 12 · Freer practice

Describe someone you know

Work in pairs. Describe a friend or someone in your family. Your partner draws them or guesses who it is. Try to use:

  1. has / has got + hair, eyes, a beard
  2. is + tall, short, bald
  3. wears glasses

Then describe someone in this room without saying their name. Can your partner guess who it is?
