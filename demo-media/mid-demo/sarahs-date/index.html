A reading lesson built on two dialogues

This is the first of the two demonstration lessons your tutors teach the A2 class on day one, and you watch it with the observation task in hand. The class talks about first dates, meets the five words they need, reads one dialogue quickly and then again closely, and finishes by talking about themselves.

The second lesson, Describing people, takes its language from Sarah's first dialogue, so watch for the sentence where she describes Jack.

Level
A2
Main aim
To give students practice in reading for gist and for specific information in the context of a first date
Subsidiary aim
To give students fluency practice in speaking about dates and going out
Watch forThe photo does the work here. Watch how the tutor asks one open question and takes ideas from the whole class before saying anything about the topic, and how little the tutor talks.

Watch forThe questions are about the students' own lives, so everyone has something to say. Watch how the pairs are set up, how long they talk before feedback, and what the tutor does with what they hear.
Step 2 · Engage

Ask and answer

In pairs, discuss, then tell the class.

  1. Where do people usually go on a first date in your country?
  2. Is online dating popular in your country?
  3. What do you talk about on a first date?
Watch forOnly five words are taught: the ones the students need to follow the text, not every new word in it. Watch how the instructions for the matching are given (shown first, then checked) and how meaning is clear before anyone reads.

        Step 6 · Bridge task

        Where should Sarah go on her second date?

        In groups, choose one place and be ready to say why.

        Watch forWatch how the tutor monitors while the groups talk: listening, noting language, and not interrupting to correct.
        Step 7 · Discuss

        Justify your choice

        1. Why do you think Sarah should go to the place you chose?
        2. What can they do there on their date?
        3. How can they make their date fun and special?
        4. Is this place good for a date in summer and in winter?
        Watch forThe students know the story by now, so the second text goes straight to detail. Notice that the last questions ask for their own opinions, which leads into the speaking.
